# Scott Travis's Drum Setup on Judas Priest's Angel of Retribution (2005)

> Inside Scott Travis's mid-2000s drum rig on Angel of Retribution — the album that marked Rob Halford's long-awaited return to Judas Priest after a 13-year departure, bridging the 15-year arc from Painkiller (1990) to Nostradamus (2008) and the 'Eulogy/Lochness' epic.

**Type:** Album Drum Setup
**Drummer(s):** [Scott Travis](/llms/drummers/scott-travis.md)
**Band / Album:** Judas Priest — *Angel of Retribution* (2005)
**Genre:** Heavy Metal

## Overview

When Rob Halford rejoined Judas Priest in 2003 after a 13-year departure that began in 1992, the metal world braced for what would either be a triumphant homecoming or an awkward nostalgia trip. "Angel of Retribution," released on February 28, 2005 through Epic Records and Sony BMG, turned out to be the former — a deliberate, song-focused reunion record that hit #1 in Germany and #13 on the Billboard 200. For Scott Travis, the album represented something subtler but equally significant: his first studio album with the classic Halford-Tipton-Downing-Hill lineup that had been his original goal when he joined the band in 1989.

Recorded at a series of California studios and co-produced by Roy Z alongside the band itself, Angel of Retribution sits in a unique place on the Judas Priest timeline. It closes the 15-year arc gap between 1990's blistering "Painkiller" and the orchestral concept work of 2008's "Nostradamus," before the band's eventual return to brute-force metal on 2018's "Firepower." Travis's drumming on the record — particularly on the 13-minute closing epic "Eulogy/Lochness" — is the connective tissue that holds those eras together.

The gear story on Angel of Retribution is one of transition. By 2005, Travis had moved off the Tama Artstar II kit and Paiste cymbals that carried him through Painkiller and Jugulator and onto Pearl's flagship Reference Series — a hybrid maple/birch/mahogany shell pack in Piano Black — paired with a new Sabian cymbal deal. Photographs and live footage from the 2004-2005 sessions and supporting tour consistently show him behind the Pearl kit, with a hand-hammered Sabian HH/AA hybrid setup and Vater sticks. Tracks like the lead single "Revolution," the anthemic "Worth Fighting For," and the sprawling "Eulogy/Lochness" each showcase a different facet of that new mid-period rig.

This is the album where Travis stopped trying to repeat Painkiller and started playing for the song. The double-kick fury is still there when the material demands it, but Angel of Retribution leans on groove, dynamics, and patience — exactly what a reunion record needed to be taken seriously rather than dismissed as a victory lap.

### Travis's Pearl Reference Series Rig

By 2005, Scott Travis had moved off the Tama Artstar II shell pack that defined his Painkiller-era sound and onto Pearl's flagship Reference Series — a hybrid maple/birch/mahogany shell pack finished, as before, in Piano Black. Photos and rehearsal footage from the Angel of Retribution sessions and the supporting 2005 tour consistently show him behind the new Pearl kit, tuned for a fundamentally different goal on this record — songs over spectacle.

For an album like Angel of Retribution, which oscillates between mid-tempo grooves on "Worth Fighting For" and double-kick blasts on "Hellrider," the Reference Series's warmer, more versatile hybrid shell paid off: the kit could speak quietly during the verses and explode without changing tuning.

Travis ran a streamlined four-tom configuration — 10" and 12" up top, 14" and 16" on the floor — a noticeable simplification from the three-rack-tom Painkiller setup. The reduced tom count let him commit to bigger, more architectural fills rather than the rapid-fire melodic runs of 1990. The dual 22" x 18" bass drums (two inches deeper than the 22" x 16" Painkiller-era kicks) gave him more air and a fuller bottom end suited to the album's heavier mid-2000s production aesthetic.

### Signature Authority

For Angel of Retribution, Travis paired his new Reference Series kit with a Pearl Reference Brass snare — a 14" x 6.5" beaded brass drum with die-cast hoops that replaced the steel snare he'd used through the Painkiller and Jugulator era.

The brass shell gives the snare a darker, more complex backbeat with more body than the earlier steel drum — "Worth Fighting For," "Eulogy," and the closing stretch of "Lochness" all benefit from a snare that can sustain a backbeat without disappearing. At the same time, the die-cast hoops keep rim shots sharp and tight, which matters during the double-kick passages on "Judas Rising" and "Hellrider" where Travis still needs Painkiller-style snap on the 2 and 4.

Travis tuned the drum medium-high — the same tuning philosophy he's used across his career — to find a middle ground between projection and tone. The brass snare's darker character is a big part of what makes Angel of Retribution feel modern next to its 1990 predecessor while still sounding unmistakably like the same drummer.

### Sabian HH/AA: A New Cymbal Deal

Scott Travis's Pearl deal came with a new cymbal endorsement, and Angel of Retribution captures him early into the Sabian relationship, having moved on from his long-running Paiste setup. The rig combines hand-hammered HH cymbals (built for warmth and complexity) with machine-hammered AA cymbals (brighter, faster response) — a hybrid approach that gives Travis tonal options without sacrificing the projection he needs to sit on top of K.K. Downing and Glenn Tipton's dual-guitar wall.

The 14" HH Hi-Hats suit Angel of Retribution's groove-leaning material — particularly the verse of "Revolution" and the long instrumental passages of "Lochness," where hi-hat color matters as much as articulation.

The crash array spans 16" to 20", giving Travis a range from quick AA Medium accents to larger sustaining HH hits. The 22" HH Power Ride is the centerpiece of the right side — a heavy, articulate ride with a pronounced bell that drives the ride patterns on the verses of "Deal With the Devil" and the slow build of "Eulogy." The 18" AA China provides the trashy accent voice for the heavier moments on "Hellrider" and the climactic sections of "Lochness."

This setup represents Travis's first year on his new cymbal platform: a hand-hammered HH/AA hybrid rig with a deliberate balance between cutting attack and musical sustain, replacing the Paiste setup he'd used since joining the band.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What drum kit did Scott Travis use on Angel of Retribution?**

A: For the 2005 Angel of Retribution sessions, Scott Travis played a Pearl Reference Series shell pack — a hybrid maple/birch/mahogany shell pack in Piano Black that he'd moved onto after leaving the Tama Artstar II he used on Painkiller and Jugulator. The configuration featured dual 22 inch by 18 inch bass drums, 10 and 12 inch rack toms, and 14 and 16 inch floor toms. It's the same Pearl platform that would carry through Nostradamus and Redeemer of Souls before Travis's next gear change arrived with Firepower in 2018.

**Q: Did Scott Travis switch drum brands by 2005?**

A: Yes — Travis switched from Tama drums and Paiste cymbals to Pearl's Reference Series and a new Sabian HH/AA hybrid cymbal setup around the time of the Halford reunion. The move gave him a warmer, more versatile hybrid shell pack better suited to Angel of Retribution's dynamic range, and it's the platform documented on the album alongside a Pearl Reference Brass snare, Pearl Demon Drive pedals, and Vater signature sticks. Travis stayed on this Pearl/Sabian setup through Nostradamus (2008) and Redeemer of Souls (2014) before switching again for Firepower (2018).

**Q: What cymbals did Scott Travis use on Angel of Retribution?**

A: Travis had just moved to Sabian, and his 2005 setup combined the hand-hammered HH line with the machine-hammered AA line. The rig included 14 inch HH Hi-Hats, HH and AA crashes from 16 to 20 inches, a 22 inch HH Power Ride, and an 18 inch AA China for trashy accents on tracks like Hellrider and the Lochness section of the closing epic.

**Q: How does Angel of Retribution fit between Painkiller and Firepower in Scott Travis's career?**

A: Angel of Retribution closes a 15-year arc gap on the Judas Priest timeline: Painkiller in 1990, Angel of Retribution in 2005, Nostradamus in 2008, and Firepower in 2018. For Travis specifically, it's the album where he stops being purely the speed-metal phenom of Painkiller and becomes the long-arc Priest drummer — and where he switched from his Painkiller-era Tama/Paiste setup to a new Pearl Reference Series kit and Sabian cymbals. That Pearl/Sabian platform, and a more restrained song-serving approach, both point toward the mature drumming he'd bring to the band's later work. It's also Rob Halford's first studio album back with Judas Priest after a 13-year departure, making it as much a comeback record for the band as a maturation point for Travis's playing.

**Q: What is significant about the 'Eulogy/Lochness' track for drum performance?**

A: Eulogy/Lochness is a 13-minute closing epic that gives Travis more long-form space than almost any other Priest track. Eulogy opens as a slow, brooding ballad with minimal kit work, then Lochness detonates into a doom-paced epic that builds through tom pulses, china accents, and a climactic double-kick section. It's drumming as long-form arrangement, and it foreshadows the orchestral ambitions Priest would pursue on 2008's Nostradamus. For a 2005 reunion record that needed to prove the band still had reach, it's the showcase track.
