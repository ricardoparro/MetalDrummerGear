## Overview

Released on April 30, 2021, Gojira's seventh studio album *Fortitude* arrived as the culmination of a decade-long artistic evolution — the final chapter in a story that began with the raw, exploratory *Magma* and ended with one of the most polished, ambitious records of the band's career. Where *Magma* stripped everything back, *Fortitude* builds outward with confidence: richer arrangements, broader sonic palette, and a production philosophy that elevated every element of the band's sound.

At the center of this evolution sits Mario Duplantier's most refined setup to date. For *Fortitude*, Mario continued to refine his longtime Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit — the culmination of his long-running relationship with the brand. The result is a drum sound that combines the punch and clarity he developed through the Magma years with a new dimensional richness: deeper resonance, more complex overtones, and a physical presence that commands the record's ambitious arrangements.

The album's Grammy credential is "Amazonia" — nominated for Best Metal Performance at the 65th Grammy Awards (2023). The track is a thunderous environmental anthem, built around one of Mario's most powerful drum performances: a driving, intensely physical pattern that never sacrifices musicality for aggression. That a Grammy nomination came from such an uncompromising track is a testament to how completely Gojira had made their vision accessible without diluting it.

*Fortitude* debuted on international charts, continuing the commercial trajectory that *Magma* had established. The album was self-produced by Joe Duplantier at Silver Cord Studio in New York — but with a crucial new element: mixing by Andy Wallace, the legendary engineer behind Nirvana's *Nevermind*, Rage Against the Machine's debut, and System of a Down's *Toxicity*. Wallace's touch elevated the drum sound to a new standard of clarity and power, giving Mario's Tama kit the full-frequency impact it deserved.

This article breaks down every component of Mario Duplantier's setup on *Fortitude*, explains the gear evolution from *Magma*, and examines what the Grammy nomination for "Amazonia" reveals about his place at the summit of progressive metal drumming.

### The Tama Starclassic Bubinga Era: Dimensional Richness

Mario Duplantier's continued refinement of his Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit for *Fortitude* marks the latest step in his recorded catalog — at least through 2021 — and arguably his most deliberate. Building on the bubinga/birch hybrid shells that served *Magma*'s leaner production, the *Fortitude*-era configuration offered a new dimension: greater shell resonance, a broader tonal palette, and a physical presence suited to *Fortitude*'s more ambitious arrangements.

Tama drums are known for their engineering precision and tonal consistency. The configuration Mario employed retained his fundamental approach — dual 22" x 18" bass drums, four-tom setup — but introduced a new character to the shell tone. Where the Magma-era voicing emphasized warmth and punch, the *Fortitude* shells deliver a more complex resonance: present, three-dimensional, and capable of greater dynamic range.

Mario's dual bass drum configuration remained the constant anchor across every gear refinement. Two independent 22" kick drums — each played with a single pedal, not a double pedal — define Gojira's rhythmic identity as much as any compositional choice. The bass drum tone on *Fortitude* is particularly notable: fuller and more commanding than on *Magma*, with Andy Wallace's mixing ensuring the kick translates with physical impact across every playback system.

The four-tom configuration persisted as Mario's melodic canvas. His approach to toms — tribal patterns, pitched melodic figures, rhythmic countermelodies — demands shells that respond consistently at any tuning. The Tama Starclassic Bubinga configuration delivers this across the full range from tight rack toms to deep, resonant floor toms. On tracks like "Born for One Thing" and "Amazonia," the toms are not fill vehicles but melodic statements — extensions of the composition itself.

### Precision and Authority on 'Amazonia'

Mario's snare on *Fortitude* is a Tama S.L.P. G-Maple, matched to the kit configuration. The 14" x 6.5" dimensions align with his long-standing preference — a size that delivers authority in the heaviest passages while maintaining sensitivity at the dynamic lower end.

The diagonal-grain maple construction provides the clarity and definition that *Fortitude*'s polished production demands. Where the snare voicing on *Magma* was warm and slightly dark, the *Fortitude*-era tuning delivers a cleaner, more direct attack — suited to a mix where Andy Wallace's engineering maximizes the punch of each stroke without adding artificial sheen.

On "Amazonia" — the Grammy-nominated track — the snare is the rhythmic spine of one of Mario's most driving performances. The backbeat lands with authority throughout the track's relentless central groove, every rim shot translating as a physical statement rather than a technical choice. The S.L.P. G-Maple's response time enables the complexity Mario deploys: ghost notes beneath the groove, sudden explosive accents at phrase peaks, and the ability to shift from restraint to full power within a single bar.

*Fortitude*'s production, courtesy of Joe Duplantier and Andy Wallace's mixing, gives the snare remarkable clarity. The drum sits in the center of the mix with presence and definition — not buried, not harsh, but exactly present enough to anchor every rhythmic figure across twelve tracks.

### Zildjian Endorsement Continues: Evolved K/A Custom Setup

Mario Duplantier's Zildjian endorsement continued into the *Fortitude* era, maintaining the tonal philosophy he established on *Magma* while serving a more ambitious and polished production. The K Custom/A Custom hybrid — hand-hammered from B20 bronze — remained his cymbal of choice: full, musical, and present without harshness.

For *Fortitude*, the cymbal setup's primary function shifted slightly from *Magma*'s direct, stripped-back production. Where *Magma* used the K/A Custom hybrid's warmth to fill a leaner mix, *Fortitude*'s more layered arrangements demand cymbals that project clearly across denser textures. The Zildjian setup's balanced, full-bodied character — present and projecting without dominating — proved equally suited to this context.

The 14" K Sweet hi-hats provide the controlled, musical foundation for Mario's groove work. On "Born for One Thing" — the album's relentless opening statement — the hi-hats drive the main groove with authority, each stroke clearly articulated beneath the central guitar riff. The K Sweet hi-hats' weight and character maintain definition at high tempos without becoming harsh.

The crash configuration — 17", 19", and 20" K Custom/A Custom crashes — gives Mario the full range of attack and sustain profiles his dynamic approach requires. The 17" A Custom Crash provides immediate, decaying accents for tight transitions; the 20" K Custom Dark Crash sustains for the musical resolution of longer phrases. Zildjian K/A Custom crashes sit in a mix rather than dominating it — serving the track rather than advertising the instrument.

Andy Wallace's mixing treated the Zildjian cymbals with the same care applied to every other element of the drum sound. The result is a cymbal presentation that is natural, dimensional, and perfectly balanced across the full frequency spectrum — a professional benchmark for modern metal production.

*Fortitude* completes the most significant arc in progressive metal drumming of the 2010s. From the raw tribal intensity of *From Mars to Sirius* through *Magma*'s stripped-back precision to *Fortitude*'s Grammy-nominated polish, Mario Duplantier's recorded journey documents how a drummer can evolve — not in technique (which was always exceptional) but in musical purpose.

The Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit on *Fortitude* represents Mario's most deliberate gear refinement. Every previous adjustment — tuning and voicing changes within his long-running Tama/Zildjian setup — aligned with a specific sonic and philosophical direction in Gojira's music. The *Fortitude* era's dimensional character, combined with Andy Wallace's mixing, produced drums that communicate with the listener at a physical level: not just audible but felt.

"Amazonia"'s Grammy nomination is the official recognition. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What drum kit did Mario Duplantier use on Fortitude?**

A: On Fortitude (2021), Mario Duplantier played a Tama Starclassic Bubinga drum kit, continuing to refine the same kit used on Magma (2016). His configuration maintained the signature Gojira approach: two independent 22" x 18" bass drums (single pedals, not a double pedal), 10" and 12" rack toms, and 14" and 16" floor toms. The Tama Starclassic Bubinga setup paired with Zildjian K Custom/A Custom cymbals and Tama hardware. Andy Wallace's mixing on Fortitude gave the Bubinga kit its full dimensional impact on record — the most sonically complete drum sound of Mario's recorded career to date.

**Q: Was "Amazonia" from Fortitude nominated for a Grammy?**

A: Yes. "Amazonia" by Gojira, from the album Fortitude (2021), was nominated for Best Metal Performance at the 65th Grammy Awards (2023). The track is an environmental anthem and features one of Mario Duplantier's most physically powerful recorded drum performances — a relentless, anthemic groove that drives the track's message with uncompromising intensity. The nomination followed Gojira's first Grammy nomination for "Silvera" from Magma at the 59th Grammy Awards (2017), establishing the band as consistent Grammy contenders in the metal performance category.

**Q: How does the Fortitude drum setup compare to the Magma era?**

A: The Fortitude drum setup represents a refinement of the same setup used on Magma. Mario continued playing the Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit (Magma and Fortitude alike) but with a more dimensional voicing — a refinement that introduced greater dimensional resonance and tonal complexity suited to Fortitude's more ambitious arrangements. Cymbals remained Zildjian K Custom/A Custom (continuity from Magma). Hardware remained Tama Iron Cobra pedals throughout. The most transformative addition, however, was Andy Wallace as mixing engineer — his work on Fortitude elevated the drum sound to a new professional standard compared to Magma's Joe Duplantier-mixed approach, giving every element of the Tama Starclassic Bubinga setup maximum sonic impact.

**Q: Who produced and mixed the drums on Fortitude?**

A: Fortitude was self-produced by Joe Duplantier at Silver Cord Studio in New York — continuing Gojira's tradition of complete creative control. The key difference from previous albums was the addition of Andy Wallace as mixing engineer. Wallace, whose credits include Nirvana's Nevermind, Rage Against the Machine's debut album, System of a Down's Toxicity, and Jeff Buckley's Grace, brought world-class mixing expertise that gave Mario's Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit its maximum sonic potential. The combination of Joe's intimate production knowledge and Wallace's mix engineering produced the most polished and powerful drum sound in Gojira's catalog.
