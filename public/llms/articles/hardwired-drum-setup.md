# Lars Ulrich's Drum Setup on Metallica's Hardwired...to Self-Destruct (2016)

> Lars Ulrich's most uptempo Metallica recording since the Black Album era — how the Tama Starclassic Maple kit, Zildjian cymbals, and Tama Speed Cobra pedals powered the Hardwired sessions at Metallica's HQ studio.

**Type:** Album Drum Setup

**Band / Album:** Metallica — *Hardwired...to Self-Destruct* (2016)
**Genre:** Thrash Metal

## Overview

Released on November 18, 2016, "Hardwired...to Self-Destruct" was Metallica's tenth studio album and the band's most uptempo recording since the Black Album era. An ambitious double album spread across two discs and twelve tracks, Hardwired debuted at #1 in 57 countries — confirming that even three decades into their career, Metallica still commanded the global metal conversation. The lead single, "Hardwired" itself, was a sub-three-minute thrash assault that signaled the band's intent: more speed, more aggression, and a deliberate reach back toward the energy of their classic-era output.

For Lars Ulrich, Hardwired marked a return to relentless tempos. After the slower, more atmospheric "Death Magnetic" (2008) sessions and the long touring cycle that followed, Lars approached the new material with the most sustained uptempo playing he had committed to tape since the early 1990s. Tracks like "Spit Out the Bone," "Atlas, Rise!," and the title track demand the kind of double-bass stamina that Lars had relied on during the "...And Justice for All" era — and the recording captures him in remarkable form.

The production setup itself was a significant departure from previous Metallica records. Where "Death Magnetic" had Rick Rubin at the helm and "Load"/"Reload"/"Black Album" were Bob Rock productions, Hardwired was co-produced by Greg Fidelman alongside James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich themselves. The album was recorded at HQ — Metallica's own studio facility in San Rafael, California — giving the band complete control over the sonic environment and the time to dial in exactly the drum sound they wanted.

That HQ environment, combined with Fidelman's punchy, modern production approach, produced a drum tone that sat squarely in the lineage of "Death Magnetic" but with a slightly fuller, warmer character. Lars continued playing his Tama Starclassic Maple kit through this era — the same brand and series that had carried him from Load through Death Magnetic — now in a dual-kick configuration that gave Hardwired's most aggressive passages a low-end weight the single-kick Death Magnetic setup lacked.

This article covers Lars's complete gear setup for the Hardwired sessions, the HQ studio recording philosophy, and how this album fits within the broader Black Album → Death Magnetic → Hardwired arc of Lars's modern career.

### Lars's Hardwired Kit: Tama Starclassic Maple

For Hardwired...to Self-Destruct, Lars Ulrich anchored his setup on the Tama Starclassic Maple kit — the same brand and series he had used since the Load era, now in a Deeper Purple finish and a dual-kick configuration. Where the earlier Tama Starclassic Birch of Death Magnetic emphasized attack and punch, the Starclassic Maple offered a wider tonal palette: warmer fundamentals, longer sustain, and the kind of fullness that suited Hardwired's heavier, riff-dense arrangements.

The defining choice for Hardwired was the move to dual 22" bass drums. Lars had spent decades running a single 22" kick with an Iron Cobra double pedal, but the dual-kick configuration on Hardwired delivered tonal consistency and visual presence that matched the album's renewed thrash ambitions. Each bass drum could be tuned independently, allowing subtle character differences between the two, and the dual-kick rig eliminated the slight mechanical compromises inherent to slave-beater double pedals. This same Starclassic Maple configuration, in Deeper Purple, became the basis for the "Worldwired" touring kit sold as a limited-edition Tama replica.

Tama's thin maple shell construction — 6-ply for the toms, 8-ply for the kicks — combined with the Star-cast mounting system (which lets shells resonate freely rather than being clamped by traditional lugs) produced a kit with more low-end body than the Starclassic Birch of Death Magnetic: fuller toms, kick drums with deeper thump, and a snare drum that sat in a more cushioned environment.

Lars kept his tom configuration economical for Hardwired: two rack toms (8" and 10") up top with 14" and 16" floor toms below. This is the same minimal-tom philosophy he has favored since the Justice era — Lars's playing has always emphasized kick-snare interaction over tom flurries, and the limited tom array reflects that priority. When the toms do appear, as in the fills throughout "Atlas, Rise!" and the explosive moments in "Spit Out the Bone," they punctuate rather than decorate.

Greg Fidelman worked closely with Lars during pre-production at HQ studio to dial in consistent tuning across every drum. Combined with Evans heads tuned for attack and the room treatment Metallica had developed at their own facility, the kit sound on Hardwired stands among the most refined Lars has ever committed to record.

### Tama's Steel Plate Snare: Hardwired's Cracking Voice

For Hardwired, Lars Ulrich's snare drum was one of Tama's signature steel plate snares — the model DRUM! Magazine identified as the drum engineer Greg Fidelman settled on after auditioning around 15 candidate snares during pre-production at HQ. Because Lars's forceful playing style detuned the drum over the course of a session, the band kept four or five identical steel plate snares in rotation, swapping and retuning between takes to keep the tone consistent across the record.

The steel shell delivered a bright, cutting attack that registered clearly through the densely layered guitar parts on tracks like "Moth Into Flame" and "Halo on Fire," while die-cast hoops kept rimshots tight and consistent throughout the album's most punishing passages. The steel's natural brightness balanced well against the Zildjian cymbal array, producing a snare voice that cut without disappearing into the mix — exactly the tonal balance Hardwired's mix demanded.

Fidelman's snare capture at HQ combined three close-mounted microphones with the room's natural ambience, producing what DRUM! Magazine described as an exceptionally resonant tone that cuts through the mix. The HQ studio's controlled acoustic environment, which the band had refined across years of pre-production work in the facility, gave the snare a natural room character without bleed problems.

Lars's tuning approach for Hardwired stayed in the medium-high range that has defined his playing for decades: enough tension for sharp attack and clarity in fast passages, with the resonant head tuned slightly looser to give the snare body. Evans Heavyweight batter head delivered the durability needed for the album's heaviest backbeats without sacrificing tonal response.

### The Zildjian Z Custom and A Custom Arsenal

Lars's cymbal setup for Hardwired blended the Zildjian A Custom series — his go-to range since the Death Magnetic era — with select Z Custom pieces for added weight. Zildjian has been Lars's endorsement since the Master of Puppets era, and by 2016 the A Custom range had become his established core, with Z Custom crashes added for tracks demanding extra projection.

The 14" A Custom hi-hats remained the rhythmic anchor of the setup. Their tight, bright character cut through the densely layered guitars on tracks like "Hardwired" and "Atlas, Rise!," delivering the kind of cutting 8th-note definition that uptempo thrash drumming requires. The A Custom's brilliant finish gave the hats a presence in the mix that traditional A Series hats lacked.

The crash array on Hardwired expanded slightly versus the Death Magnetic setup. A 16" A Custom delivered fast accents, an 18" Z Custom contributed heavier projection for major section endings, and a 19" A Custom provided full-wash sustain for climactic moments throughout "Spit Out the Bone" and "Halo on Fire." The Z Custom's heavier weight versus the A Custom gave Lars a wider dynamic palette than relying on a single series.

The 20" A Custom Ride carried the rhythmic load during the ride-driven passages on "Moth Into Flame" and the instrumental sections of "Halo on Fire," its clear bell definition cutting through the mix without sacrificing wash sustain. The 18" A Custom China supplied the explosive, trashy accents that have been a Metallica signature since "Master of Puppets" — used for riff endings and dramatic punctuation throughout the album.

The brilliant finish across the A Custom range produced a slightly more aggressive attack profile than traditional A Series cymbals would have delivered — a tonal choice that matched Greg Fidelman's punchy, modern production approach at HQ.

## Hardwired: The Modern Lars Ulrich Configuration

Hardwired...to Self-Destruct captures Lars Ulrich at a confident moment in his modern career — playing the most sustained uptempo material Metallica had recorded since the Black Album era, on a refined setup that combined Tama shells and hardware, Zildjian cymbals, Evans heads, and Ahead sticks into a single coherent rig. The album sits cleanly within the Black Album → Death Magnetic → Hardwired arc: each record represents Lars at a particular gear and production juncture, all built around the same Tama-exclusive drum endorsement he has held since the mid-1980s.

The Tama Starclassic Maple kit, dual 22" bass drums, and Tama Speed Cobra pedals became Lars's established touring rig from 2016 onward — through the WorldWired Tour and into Metallica's subsequent activity, with Tama later selling a limited-edition "Worldwired" replica of the kit. The combination of self-production at HQ studio and Greg Fidelman's punchy modern engineering produced a drum tone that avoided the Loudness War controversy of Death Magnetic while delivering the kind of weight and attack that the material demanded.

For drummers studying Lars Ulrich's modern setup, the Hardwired configuration is the reference. The gear is achievable: Tama Starclassic Maple remains in production, Zildjian A Custom and Z Custom cymbals are widely available, Tama Speed Cobra HP910 pedals remain a standard choice for working drummers, and Evans heads are everywhere. What's less replicable is the context — three decades of Metallica drumming refined into the configuration that worked, and a self-owned studio environment with unlimited time for sound refinement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What drums did Lars Ulrich use on Hardwired...to Self-Destruct?**

A: Lars Ulrich recorded Hardwired...to Self-Destruct in 2016 using his Tama Starclassic Maple drum kit, finished in Deeper Purple — the same brand and series he had used since the Load era, now in a dual-kick configuration. The setup included dual 22"x16" bass drums, two rack toms (8" and 10"), and 14" and 16" floor toms. The Starclassic Maple's thin 6-ply (toms) / 8-ply (kick) shells and Star-cast mounting delivered a warmer, fuller tone than the Tama Starclassic Birch he used on Death Magnetic — a fit for Greg Fidelman's punchier production at Metallica's HQ studio in San Rafael, California. This same kit and finish later became the basis for Tama's limited-edition "Worldwired" touring replica.

**Q: What snare did Lars Ulrich use on Hardwired?**

A: Lars Ulrich used one of Tama's signature steel plate snares on Hardwired — engineer Greg Fidelman auditioned around 15 candidate snares before settling on the model, and the band kept four or five identical units in rotation since Lars's forceful playing detuned the drum quickly. The steel shell delivered a bright, cutting attack, with die-cast hoops keeping rimshots tight and consistent. The snare was tuned medium-high for attack and clarity, paired with an Evans Heavyweight batter head for durability under the album's aggressive backbeats.

**Q: What cymbals did Lars Ulrich use on Hardwired?**

A: Lars Ulrich used a mix of Zildjian A Custom and Z Custom cymbals on Hardwired. His setup included 14 inch A Custom hi-hats, 16 inch and 19 inch A Custom crashes, an 18 inch Z Custom crash for added projection, a 20 inch A Custom ride, and an 18 inch A Custom China. Zildjian has been Lars's endorsement since the Master of Puppets era, and the A Custom range became his established core during the Death Magnetic period, with Z Custom pieces added on Hardwired for heavier projection.

**Q: What pedals did Lars Ulrich use on Hardwired?**

A: Lars Ulrich used Tama Speed Cobra HP910 pedals for the Hardwired era, continuing the pedal line he had used since the Death Magnetic touring cycle. The Speed Cobra's long footboard and Cobra Coil spring system deliver fast rebound and effortless response — well suited to the sustained double-bass passages on tracks like "Spit Out the Bone" and "Atlas, Rise!." Used as two single pedals on a dual-kick rig, the Speed Cobras gave Lars consistent feel across both feet at the demanding uptempo arrangements Hardwired required.

**Q: Where was Hardwired...to Self-Destruct recorded?**

A: Hardwired...to Self-Destruct was recorded at HQ — Metallica's own studio facility in San Rafael, California. The album was self-produced by James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich alongside Greg Fidelman, who had worked extensively with the band since the Death Magnetic era as engineer and mixer. Recording at HQ gave the band complete environmental control and the kind of unlimited time for drum-sound refinement that conventional rented studio environments cannot match. The album debuted at #1 in 57 countries upon its November 18, 2016 release.

**Q: How does Hardwired compare to Death Magnetic for drum sound?**

A: Hardwired sits in a clear lineage with Death Magnetic but represents an evolution rather than a repeat. Where Death Magnetic used a Tama Starclassic Birch kit with a single bass drum + double pedal and a steel Tama LU1465 snare under Rick Rubin's production, Hardwired moved to a Tama Starclassic Maple kit with dual 22 inch bass drums and a Tama steel plate snare under Greg Fidelman's co-production. The Hardwired drum tone has more low-end weight and slightly fuller body than Death Magnetic, but maintains the same attack-forward, thrash-aligned character. Both albums avoid the over-compressed mastering issues that plagued Death Magnetic's release.
