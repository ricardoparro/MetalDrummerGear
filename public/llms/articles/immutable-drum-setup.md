## Overview

Released on April 1, 2022 on Atomic Fire Records, Meshuggah's tenth studio album *Immutable* arrived after a six-year gap that included a global pandemic, a record-label change, and an extended period of settling into a road-tested rig for Tomas Haake. The COVID-enforced delay — originally scheduled for 2020 — gave the band, and Haake specifically, more time than any previous album cycle to finesse every detail of the recording setup.

The result is Meshuggah's most sonically precise modern album and the clearest snapshot of Tomas Haake's current drum rig. Where *The Violent Sleep of Reason* (2016) showcased a raw live-in-studio energy, *Immutable* returns to meticulous studio construction — with the same Sonor SQ2 Heavy Beech platform, Sabian HHX/AAX cymbals, and two independent Tama Speed Cobra single pedals that have defined Haake's sound since the obZen/Koloss era.

*Immutable* earned Meshuggah a Grammy nomination for Best Metal Performance ("The Abysmal Eye"), their first since 2013. It also marked their debut on Atomic Fire Records after decades with Nuclear Blast. For drummers and gear enthusiasts, it represents the most current and authoritative reference point for Haake's kit — the present-day answer to "what does Tomas Haake play?"

This breakdown covers every piece of gear Tomas used on *Immutable*, how the pandemic refinement period shaped the setup, and why this album closes the Meshuggah studio arc with Haake's most settled configuration to date.

### The Refined Standard: Sonor SQ2 Heavy Beech

For *Immutable*, Tomas Haake used his Sonor SQ2 Heavy Beech kit in the same Meshuggah-specific configuration he's refined since the Koloss era. The Heavy Beech shells are one of Sonor's premium shell constructions — thicker beech plies deliver more attack and volume than standard configurations, essential when battling eight-string guitars tuned to F standard.

The Heavy Beech construction delivers a distinctive tonal character: harder, more defined attack than lighter shells, with a focused low-end punch that remains articulate even at Meshuggah's extreme downtuned frequencies. For Haake's bass drum work — where individual 32nd notes must remain perceptibly discrete — this articulation is not optional.

The configuration mirrors the setup Haake used on *The Violent Sleep of Reason* and, before that, obZen and Koloss — demonstrating continuity rather than reinvention. The single 24x18 inch bass drum, driven by two independent single pedals, is tuned for maximum punch with controlled sustain — each kick note needs to speak clearly in a dense wall of low-tuned guitar and bass frequencies.

The COVID pandemic delay worked in the kit's favor. Haake had additional time to further dial in this already road-tested configuration during the extended gap between albums, arriving in the studio with a fully settled rig rather than one still being adjusted mid-cycle.

### Signature Precision: The Sonor Snare at 14x6.5

The Sonor Tomas Haake Signature snare on *Immutable* continues Haake's preference for bright, cutting snares that project through Meshuggah's dense guitar frequencies. At 14x6.5 inches, it maintains the dimensions he's used across the modern Meshuggah catalog — enough depth for body, without sacrificing the attack response Haake requires for precisely articulated snare hits.

The signature line is built to Haake's own bearing-edge specification, engineered for maximum resonance and sensitivity. Unlike heavier double-ply shells, the maple single-ply construction allows the snare to respond with immediacy — essential when every snare hit occupies a precise rhythmic position within Meshuggah's complex polyrhythmic architecture.

Haake tunes the snare medium-high with tight snare wires, maintaining the bright, cutting character that anchors the listener's sense of pulse even as guitars and bass explore extended polyrhythmic cycles. In Meshuggah's music, the snare is often the most reliable rhythmic reference point — the "clock" that grounds the listener while everything else seems to dissolve time.

### Sabian HHX/AAX Arsenal: The Established Combination

Tomas Haake's cymbal setup for *Immutable* is the same Sabian HHX/AAX combination he's used since the obZen/Koloss era — a pairing that reached its most refined form on this album rather than being replaced by something new. The HHX series provides the darker, more complex overtones Haake favors for crash and ride work; the AAX series adds brightness and quick response for accents.

The 14" HHX Compression Hi-Hats — Haake's own signature design — remain central to his pattern work. Meshuggah's groove frequently lives in the hi-hat layer — the consistent pulse against which guitars and kicks create polyrhythmic tension. The Compression Hi-Hats' quick, tight articulation ensures that pulse remains audible even in the most densely arranged passages.

The HHX 19" Evolution crash provides the darker, more complex overtones that contrast with the brighter AAX X-Plosion. Darker cymbals respond more slowly than bright crashes, creating a different kind of accent — one that blooms rather than snaps. This contrast between the bright AAX crash (immediate, sharp) and the darker HHX Evolution (slower, complex) gives Haake more expressive range within the same musical context.

The HHX 18" Chinese continues Meshuggah's preference for trashy, definitive section markers. In music with extended rhythmic cycles, the China cut through precisely delineates structural moments — when Meshuggah's polyrhythm finally "resolves," the China is often the signal.

## The Present-Day Blueprint: Haake's Most Settled Modern Setup

*Immutable* closes the Meshuggah studio arc as the definitive reference point for Tomas Haake's current drum configuration. From the Sonor Designer Series that powered obZen's genre-defining polyrhythms, through the Sonor SQ2 Heavy Beech that anchored the live-in-studio *Violent Sleep of Reason*, to the same SQ2 Heavy Beech kit that anchors *Immutable* — each album has represented gradual refinement of a single Sonor/Sabian/Tama Speed Cobra platform, not a series of brand-switching gear changes.

The COVID delay, frustrating as it was, produced an unexpected dividend: Haake arrived at Sweetspot Studios with the most thoroughly settled drum rig of any album in the band's catalog. The two Tama Speed Cobra single pedals were completely dialed in. The Sabian HHX/AAX cymbal setup was second nature. The Sonor signature snare tuning was established. Everything was ready.

For drummers studying the Meshuggah catalog, *Immutable* is the essential final chapter — the present-day answer to "what does Tomas Haake actually play?" It's also proof that after more than three decades with Meshuggah, his evolution continues within a stable platform: refined snare dimensions, dialed-in cymbal pairings, and the same foundational principle that has defined his playing since "Bleed" — two independent single pedals, never a double pedal.

Groove is everything. Complexity serves the song. The polyrhythms are not math exercises — they are music.

Tomas Haake on *Immutable*: the Atomic Clock, still running.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What does Tomas Haake play on Immutable?**

A: On Meshuggah's Immutable (2022), Tomas Haake played his established Sonor SQ2 Heavy Beech custom kit in Meshuggah-spec configuration — a single 24x18 inch bass drum driven by two independent single pedals, 10x8, 12x9, and 14x12 inch rack toms, and 16x16 and 18x18 inch floor toms. His snare was a Sonor Tomas Haake Signature at 14x6.5 inches. The Heavy Beech shells deliver the hard, articulate attack essential for Meshuggah's downtuned, polyrhythmic music. This configuration is the same platform he used on The Violent Sleep of Reason (2016) and, before that, Koloss and obZen — further dialed in during the COVID-enforced production delay.

**Q: What cymbals did Meshuggah use on Immutable?**

A: Tomas Haake used his established Sabian HHX/AAX setup on Immutable: Sabian HHX 14 inch Compression Hi-Hats (his own signature design), a Sabian AAX 18 inch X-Plosion crash, a Sabian HHX 19 inch Evolution crash, a Sabian HHX 21 inch Raw Bell Dry Ride, and a Sabian HHX 18 inch Chinese. The HHX series provides the darker, more complex overtones Haake favors, while the AAX series adds brightness and quick response for accents — the same dual-line approach he's used since the obZen/Koloss era.

**Q: How does Immutable differ from obZen gear-wise?**

A: Immutable (2022) and obZen (2008) represent fourteen years of steady refinement, not gear-switching, for Tomas Haake. On obZen, Haake played Sonor Designer Series maple/beech shells with Sabian AAX/AA cymbals and two independent Sonor Perfect Balance single pedals. By Immutable, the kit had evolved to the Sonor SQ2 Heavy Beech platform (harder attack, denser shell) he adopted during the Koloss era, cymbals had shifted to Sabian's HHX/AAX combination (including his signature HHX Compression Hi-Hats), and the pedals evolved to two independent Tama Speed Cobra single pedals — but the two-single-pedal philosophy itself never changed. The Porter & Davies BC2 throne and Remo head selection remain consistent across both albums — anchors in a setup that evolved gradually rather than switching brands wholesale.

**Q: Did Tomas Haake change his pedal setup for Immutable?**

A: No — Immutable continues Haake's long-standing pedal philosophy. For his entire career, including obZen and every album since, Haake has used two completely independent Tama Speed Cobra single pedals rather than a double pedal, prioritizing absolute foot independence with no mechanical linkage between his feet. The COVID production delay gave him additional time to keep that already-dialed-in feel fully embedded in his muscle memory before entering the studio.

**Q: What album is Immutable on and when was it released?**

A: Immutable is Meshuggah's tenth studio album, released on April 1, 2022 on Atomic Fire Records — their first album on the label after decades with Nuclear Blast. The album was originally planned for release around 2020 but was delayed by the COVID-19 pandemic, which inadvertently gave Tomas Haake additional time to refine his drum setup and integrate new hardware. The album earned a Grammy nomination for Best Metal Performance for the track "The Abysmal Eye" at the 2023 Grammy Awards.
