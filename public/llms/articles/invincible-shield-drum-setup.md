# Judas Priest "Invincible Shield" Drum Setup (Scott Travis, 2024)

> Complete breakdown of Scott Travis's drum setup on Judas Priest's Invincible Shield (2024) — the band's highest-charting UK album ever (#2), Grammy-nominated for "Crown of Horns," and Travis's first Priest album in six years after Firepower. Covers his long-standing ddrum kit, Paiste RUDE/2002 cymbals, and the distributed, city-by-city recording process behind the album.

## Overview

Released on March 8, 2024 through Epic Records (Columbia in Japan two days earlier), *Invincible Shield* is Judas Priest's nineteenth studio album and Scott Travis's first Priest studio record since 2018's *Firepower*. It debuted at #2 on the UK Albums Chart — the highest chart position Judas Priest has ever reached in their home country — and opened at #18 on the Billboard 200 while topping Billboard's Top Hard Rock Albums chart. The album's centerpiece single, "Crown of Horns," earned Judas Priest a nomination for the Grammy Award for Best Metal Performance in 2025, extending a run of Grammy recognition that now spans *Redeemer of Souls* (2014), *Firepower* (2018), and *Invincible Shield* (2024).

For a band fifty-plus years into its existence, and for a drummer thirty-five years into his tenure, *Invincible Shield* is a remarkable outlier: not a victory lap, but the highest-charting album of the band's career. Scott Travis — who joined Priest in 1989 and redefined the band's drumming vocabulary on *Painkiller* the following year — remains the connective tissue across every era this arc represents, from the speed-metal explosion of 1990 through the reunion record *Angel of Retribution* (2005) to this 2024 high-water mark.

*Invincible Shield* was produced primarily by Andy Sneap, continuing the partnership established on *Firepower*, with Tom Allom returning to co-produce the album's closing two tracks, "Sons of Thunder" and "Giants in the Sky." The record's production story is unusual for a modern metal album: rather than tracking together in a single studio, the band worked in near-total geographic isolation. Travis recorded his drum parts alongside guitarist Richie Faulkner in Nashville, Tennessee. Rob Halford tracked vocals separately in Phoenix, Arizona. Bassist Ian Hill contributed his parts while on tour in Europe. Sneap then assembled the pieces and re-amped guitar parts at his own Backstage Studios in England. The album that resulted sounds cohesive and heavy despite never having all four core members in the same room during tracking — a testament to Sneap's production and to how tightly Travis's foundational drum tracks locked the arrangements together for everyone recording around them.

Gear-wise, *Invincible Shield* documents Travis on the same long-standing ddrum Dominion Series kit and Paiste RUDE/2002 cymbals he's used since *Painkiller*, the same rig he used on *Firepower* six years earlier. This article covers that setup in full and what made his drumming on this era-defining record land as hard as it did.

### The ddrum Dominion Series Era Continues

By the *Invincible Shield* era, Scott Travis was still on the same ddrum Dominion Series shell pack he used on *Firepower* (2018) and every Priest album back to *Painkiller* — the primary recording and touring kit for his entire long-standing ddrum endorsement.

The two independent 22"x18" bass drums carry over directly from the *Firepower* configuration — Travis has run two separate kick drums, rather than a single drum with a connected double pedal, since the *Painkiller* era. The shell pack's low-mid warmth suits *Invincible Shield*'s more anthemic, riff-driven material; tracks like the title track and "Panic Attack" benefit from bass drums that carry weight without losing the machine-gun clarity Travis's speed passages require.

The compact four-tom layout (10"/12" rack, 14"/16" floor) is unchanged from every Travis Priest kit since *Angel of Retribution* (2005) — a deliberate, mature choice that favors fluency and architectural fills over a sprawling tom array. The Piano Black finish keeps the visual continuity with every prior Travis-era Priest kit dating back to the *Painkiller* Dominion Series.

### ddrum Scott Travis Signature

Travis's snare for *Invincible Shield* is the same ddrum Scott Travis Signature, 14"x6.5", he's used across his entire Priest career, built to his own specifications and producing a sharp, focused crack with a pronounced high-frequency lift that helps the drum sit forward in Andy Sneap's tight, modern mix.

Die-cast hoops preserve the rim-shot clarity he relies on for the album's frequent backbeat accents, particularly on "Panic Attack" and "Devil in Disguise," where the snare needs to read clearly against Faulkner and Tipton's layered guitar tracks.

Travis continues tuning medium-high with tight snare wires — the same fundamental approach documented on every Priest album since *Painkiller* — prioritizing cut and articulation over sustained ring.

### Paiste RUDE and 2002: The Same Voice for Priest

*Invincible Shield* continues Scott Travis's long-running Paiste RUDE/2002 endorsement, the same cymbal voice that carried him through *Firepower* and every Priest album back to *Painkiller*. The RUDE line is engineered for fast response and pronounced high-frequency cut, which suits Andy Sneap's dry, upfront modern production approach.

The 14" RUDE Hi-Hats give Travis a tight, immediate chick, useful for the driving hi-hat patterns that carry the verses of "Panic Attack" and "The Serpent and the King." The crash range — 16" through 19" — favors quick attack and fast decay, letting Travis punctuate Sneap's tightly arranged riffs without cymbal wash bleeding into the next phrase.

The 22" RUDE Power Ride anchors the album's more mid-tempo, groove-driven tracks like "Trial by Fire," delivering clear stick definition and a bell that cuts for accents without overwhelming Ian Hill's bass lines. The 18" RUDE China supplies the trashy accent voice Travis has used across every Priest era since *Painkiller*.

## The Highest Peak, Thirty-Five Years In

*Invincible Shield* arrived in March 2024 as proof that Judas Priest's creative and commercial ceiling hadn't been reached decades ago. A #2 UK chart debut — the band's best ever — a Grammy nomination for "Crown of Horns," and the third consecutive Priest album to earn major awards recognition: these are not the metrics of a legacy act coasting on nostalgia.

For Scott Travis, *Invincible Shield* closes a remarkable arc. From the ddrum Dominion Series shell pack that powered *Painkiller*'s speed-metal revolution in 1990, through *Angel of Retribution*, *Nostradamus*, *Redeemer of Souls*, and *Firepower*, to the same setup documented here on *Invincible Shield* — the same long-standing ddrum and Paiste rig anchors every chapter of Priest's history Travis has played on.

What's remarkable about *Invincible Shield* is that it required Travis to do something he'd never done before: hold an entire album together while most of his bandmates recorded their parts in different cities and countries. The drums became the skeleton the rest of the record was built around, and the album's cohesion — despite that fractured process — is a direct credit to his reliability behind the kit.

Thirty-five years after replacing Dave Holland, Scott Travis is still the reason Judas Priest's rhythm section sounds inevitable. *Invincible Shield* didn't just extend his tenure — it gave him the highest chart peak of his career on the same long-standing ddrum and Paiste rig he's trusted from the start.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What drum kit does Scott Travis use on Invincible Shield?**

A: On Judas Priest's Invincible Shield (2024), Scott Travis plays the ddrum Dominion Series shell pack in Piano Black finish — two 22"x18" bass drums, 10"x8" and 12"x9" rack toms, and 14"x14" and 16"x16" floor toms. His snare is the ddrum Scott Travis Signature at 14"x6.5" with die-cast hoops. This is the same long-standing setup he used on Firepower (2018) and every Priest album back to Painkiller (1990) — Travis has been a long-time ddrum endorser with no documented brand changes.

**Q: Was Invincible Shield Grammy-nominated?**

A: Yes. Invincible Shield's track "Crown of Horns" was nominated for the Grammy Award for Best Metal Performance in 2025. It's the third consecutive Judas Priest studio album to earn Grammy recognition, following Redeemer of Souls (2014, "Halls of Valhalla") and Firepower (2018, "Lightning Strike") — both also nominated for Best Metal Performance. The album itself debuted at #2 on the UK Albums Chart, the highest position in the band's history, and reached #18 on the Billboard 200 while topping Billboard's Top Hard Rock Albums chart.

**Q: What cymbals does Scott Travis use in 2024?**

A: For Invincible Shield, Scott Travis uses the same Paiste RUDE and 2002 series cymbals that carried him through Firepower (2018) and every Priest album back to Painkiller. His setup includes 14" RUDE Hi-Hats, RUDE and 2002 crashes from 16" to 19", a 22" RUDE Power Ride, and an 18" RUDE China.

**Q: How was Invincible Shield recorded, and how did that affect Scott Travis's drumming?**

A: Invincible Shield was recorded with the band members in different locations rather than together in one studio — Scott Travis and guitarist Richie Faulkner tracked in Nashville, Tennessee; Rob Halford recorded vocals in Phoenix, Arizona; and bassist Ian Hill contributed his parts while on tour in Europe. Producer Andy Sneap assembled the pieces and re-amped guitars at his own Backstage Studios in England. Because Travis's drum tracks were recorded early in this process, they functioned as the timing and arrangement reference for the performances layered on afterward — his session consistency was essential to making the distributed recording approach sound cohesive on the finished album.
