# George Kollias Drum Setup: Nile's 'Ithyphallic' (2007) Gear Breakdown

## Overview

Released in July 2007, "Ithyphallic" is Nile's fifth studio album and the band's first release for Nuclear Blast Records, following their departure from Relapse Records after "Annihilation of the Wicked." Neil Kernon returned to produce, tracking the album at The Sound Lab in Columbia, South Carolina and mixing at Rax Trax Recording in Chicago — the same production partnership that had defined George Kollias's explosive 2005 debut with the band.

For Kollias, "Ithyphallic" occupies a specific place in his gear timeline: it is the last Nile studio album performed in full on the Pearl Reference Series / Tama Iron Cobra HP900 configuration he debuted on "Annihilation of the Wicked." By the time Nile returned to the studio for "Those Whom the Gods Detest" in 2009, Kollias had upgraded to Pearl Reference Maple shells and Pearl Demon Drive pedals — a shift that makes "Ithyphallic" the closing statement of his earliest Nile-era equipment.

Tracks like the title cut "Ithyphallic," the relentless "As He Creates So He Destroys," and the punishing "Eat of the Dead" show a drummer refining rather than reinventing — the same blast beat vocabulary and heel-toe foundation from the debut, now delivered with two additional years of touring seasoning behind it. "Papyrus Containing the Spell to Preserve Its Possessor Against Attacks from He Who Is in the Water" remains one of the most technically discussed Nile drum tracks of the era for its rhythmic density.

This article breaks down every piece of gear George Kollias used during the "Ithyphallic" sessions, the techniques that carried over and evolved from his debut, and why this overlooked bridge album matters for understanding the complete arc of his equipment history with Nile.

## Gear Breakdown

- **Drums:** Pearl Pearl Reference Series (Maple/Birch Hybrid Shell Pack finish)
- **Snare:** Pearl Pearl Free-Floating Steel, 14" x 6.5"
- **Cymbals:** Zildjian — Zildjian A Custom Series
- **Hardware / Pedals:** Tama Iron Cobra HP900 Double Bass Drum Pedal; DW 9500 Hi-Hat Stand; Tama HT550B 1st Chair Drum Throne; Vic Firth American Classic 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador Coated (batter), Remo Ambassador Snare Side (resonant)
- **Snare tuning:** Medium-high tension, carried over unchanged from the 2005 debut sessions

### George's 2007 Kit: Pearl Reference Series, One Last Time

George Kollias carried the same Pearl Reference Series kit from "Annihilation of the Wicked" into the "Ithyphallic" sessions two years later. By 2007, the kit was thoroughly road-tested — two years of touring the debut album had let Kollias settle on tuning and head choices with a confidence that a brand-new kit simply can't offer on its first outing.

The double 22" bass drum configuration remained unchanged, still delivering the independent per-foot control that Kollias's heel-toe technique depends on. Neil Kernon's returning production team knew exactly how to mic this kit from the first album, which meant the "Ithyphallic" sessions could focus on performance rather than re-learning the instrument's recorded character from scratch.

The compact tom configuration — 10", 12", 14", 16" — again prioritized reach efficiency over tonal range, a deliberate choice for a drummer whose value proposition is sustained extreme speed rather than expansive melodic tom work. The free-floating tom mounts kept the toms resonating freely, an important detail on tracks like "Papyrus Containing the Spell to Preserve Its Possessor Against Attacks from He Who Is in the Water," where tom fills punctuate otherwise relentless blast sections.

Within two years of "Ithyphallic," this kit would be retired in favor of Pearl Reference Maple — making this album the final complete studio document of Kollias's original Nile-era Pearl Reference Series rig.

### The Snare: Steel, Unchanged from the Debut

George Kollias's snare choice on "Ithyphallic" carried directly over from "Annihilation of the Wicked": a 14" x 6.5" free-floating steel-shelled drum chosen for the bright, cutting attack that steel delivers over wood at extreme blast beat tempos. Two years of live performance had let Kollias fine-tune the exact tension that balances crack against body, and that refinement is audible across "Ithyphallic"'s densest sections.

The medium-high tuning approach from the debut persisted — enough tension for immediate articulation on every stroke, not so much that the drum's fundamental pitch thins out. Neil Kernon's engineering team again used a dual-mic approach (batter-side SM57 plus an underside mic for snare wire detail) to capture the drum's full character, a technique proven on the first album and simply repeated here with the confidence of prior success.

This same Pearl Free-Floating Steel snare, carried over unchanged since "Annihilation of the Wicked," continued into "Those Whom the Gods Detest" as well — the crack heard on "Eat of the Dead" and "Laying Fire Upon Apep" comes from this consistent 14" x 6.5" free-floating steel drum.

### The Zildjian Cymbal Arsenal: Still the Original Voice

"Ithyphallic" finds George Kollias playing the same Zildjian A Custom configuration he's used since "Annihilation of the Wicked" — a cymbal voice that would remain his setup for years to come, well past this album.

The A Custom hi-hats continued to provide the articulation Kollias's blast beats demand: clear, defined strokes even as tempos push past 240 BPM. On "As He Creates So He Destroys," the hi-hat work stays legible through sections that would smear on a lesser-quality pair.

The crash spread — 17" and 18" — gave Kollias the same dynamic range he'd relied on since his debut, providing quick accents and full-weight section endings across "Ithyphallic"'s dense arrangement. The 20" A Custom ride handled the album's more measured, hypnotic passages, particularly on the title track where Nile's Egyptological atmosphere calls for controlled restraint between blast sections.

The 18" A Custom China punctuated the album's most aggressive transitions, an unchanged texture in Kollias's sonic vocabulary.

## The Quiet Bridge Album

"Ithyphallic" rarely leads discussions of Nile's essential albums, but for anyone tracing George Kollias's gear and technique history, it fills an essential gap. It is the last complete studio document of the rig that launched his career — the Pearl Reference Series, the Tama Iron Cobra HP900, the Zildjian A Custom cymbal voice, the standard Vic Firth 5B — before the pedal and kit began giving way to the Pearl Demon Drive/Reference Maple equipment that would define the rest of his Nile tenure.

Key takeaways for drummers studying this album:

- **Consolidation matters**: "Ithyphallic" shows that refining an existing technique over a full album cycle is as valuable as introducing a new one
- **Gear continuity has a cost and a benefit**: keeping the same rig from the debut let Kollias focus entirely on performance, but the eventual Pearl transition suggests real limits were being reached
- **"Papyrus..."** remains a benchmark polyrhythmic track worth close study for drummers working on hand-foot independence
- **The Tama Iron Cobra HP900's final chapter**: this album is the last place to hear Kollias's original pedal platform before the Demon Drive era began

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What drum kit does George Kollias use on Ithyphallic?**

A: On Nile's 'Ithyphallic' (2007, Nuclear Blast), George Kollias played the same Pearl Reference Series kit he used on 'Annihilation of the Wicked' (2005) — a double 22" kick drum setup with 10/12" rack toms and 14/16" floor toms. This is the final Nile studio album on this configuration before Kollias upgraded to Pearl Reference Maple shells on 'Those Whom the Gods Detest' (2009). See the earlier album's breakdown: [Annihilation of the Wicked drum setup](/articles/george-kollias-nile-annihilation-drum-setup).

**Q: How fast does George Kollias play on Ithyphallic?**

A: George Kollias sustains blast beats in the 220–260 BPM range across 'Ithyphallic,' with tracks like 'Papyrus Containing the Spell to Preserve Its Possessor Against Attacks from He Who Is in the Water' and 'As He Creates So He Destroys' representing the album's most demanding sustained passages. Kollias is Guinness World Record-certified for sustaining 280 BPM single strokes — by 2007, two years of touring 'Annihilation of the Wicked' had already sharpened the consistency behind that speed. Full technique breakdown: [George Kollias at MetalForge](/drummer/george-kollias).

**Q: What cymbals and pedals does George Kollias use on Ithyphallic?**

A: George Kollias played Zildjian A Custom cymbals on 'Ithyphallic' (2007) — 14" hi-hats, a 17"/18" crash pair, a 20" ride, and an 18" China, identical to his debut-album setup. His bass drum pedal was the Tama Iron Cobra HP900 double pedal, also carried over from 'Annihilation of the Wicked.' The pedal would be replaced during the transition to Pearl Demon Drive gear captured on 'Those Whom the Gods Detest' (2009).

**Q: Why is Ithyphallic significant in George Kollias's gear history?**

A: 'Ithyphallic' (2007) is the final Nile studio album performed in full on George Kollias's original Pearl Reference Series / Tama Iron Cobra HP900 rig — the equipment that launched his career on 'Annihilation of the Wicked' (2005). It closes out his earliest Nile-era gear chapter before the Pearl Reference Maple and Demon Drive transition documented on 'Those Whom the Gods Detest' (2009). For the full gear evolution, see: [What's in George Kollias's Kit](/articles/whats-in-george-kollias-kit).

**Q: What is Nile's Ithyphallic and why does it matter?**

A: 'Ithyphallic' (2007) is Nile's fifth studio album and the band's first release for Nuclear Blast Records, produced by Neil Kernon at The Sound Lab in Columbia, South Carolina. For drumming specifically, it represents George Kollias consolidating the technical vocabulary he introduced on 'Annihilation of the Wicked' — tighter execution, deeper endurance, and the same gear, one final time.
