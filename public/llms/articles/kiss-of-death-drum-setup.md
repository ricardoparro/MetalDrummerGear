# Kiss of Death Drum Setup: Mikkey Dee's Gear on Motörhead's 2006 Album

> Discover the exact drum kit, cymbals, and gear Mikkey Dee used on Motörhead's Kiss of Death (2006) — recorded on his longtime Sonor SQ2 kit. Complete breakdown of the Sonor SQ2 setup, Paiste cymbals, and the band's highest German chart entry to date.

**Type:** Album Drum Setup

**Band / Album:** Motörhead — *Kiss of Death* (2006)
**Genre:** Heavy Metal / Speed Metal

## Overview

Released on August 29, 2006, *Kiss of Death* is Motörhead's fifteenth studio album, tracked on Mikkey Dee's longtime Sonor SQ2 kit — an endorsement dating back to his King Diamond days — the same setup he would refine and rely on all the way through *Bad Magic* (2015).

Recorded across Paramount Recording Studios, NRG Studios, and Maple Studios in the Hollywood area, *Kiss of Death* reunited the band with producer Cameron Webb, who had steered *Inferno* two years earlier. The album became Motörhead's ninth release for Steamhammer/SPV and, notably, charted at #4 in Germany — the band's highest German chart position up to that point in its career — alongside a UK entry at #45, a Switzerland entry at #26, and a Billboard 200 appearance at #82.

The album's thirteen tracks (twelve originals plus a 2006 re-recording of the fan-favorite "R.A.M.O.N.E.S") also brought a notable cast of guest musicians into the studio: Poison guitarist C.C. DeVille contributed a guitar solo, Alice in Chains bassist Mike Inez added additional bass work, and Ignite/H2O vocalist Zoltán Téglás lent backing vocals. For an album featuring that much outside talent, Dee's drumming remained the connective tissue holding everything together — the same economical, powerhouse pocket playing that had defined his Motörhead tenure since *Bastards*, and one of the reasons the band could absorb so many outside voices without losing its identity.

Critically, *Kiss of Death* was also the last Motörhead album to feature original cover artwork from longtime band artist Joe Petagno in its initial design generation, closing out an era of the band's visual identity. This article breaks down Dee's Sonor SQ2 kit, the continuing Paiste cymbal setup, and the hardware behind one of Motörhead's most acclaimed mid-2000s records.

## Gear Breakdown

- **Drums:** Sonor Sonor SQ2 (Piano black lacquer finish)
- **Snare:** Sonor Sonor SQ2 Steel Snare, 14" x 6.5"
- **Cymbals:** Paiste — Paiste 2002 / Paiste Signature
- **Hardware / Pedals:** DW 5000 Series Double Pedal; Sonor SQ2 Hi-Hat Stand; Roc-N-Soc Nitro Throne; Vic Firth 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Emperor Coated (batter), Remo Ambassador Snare Side (resonant)
- **Snare tuning:** Medium-high tension for crack and projection

### Sonor SQ2: Dee's Settled Motörhead Foundation

*Kiss of Death* captures Mikkey Dee's Sonor SQ2 kit — a setup that had long been the foundation of his sound and would continue to be refined further across *Motörizer* (2008), *The Wörld Is Yours* (2010), and *Aftershock* (2013) before reaching its final form on *Bad Magic* (2015). The Sonor SQ2's birch shells offered a full, resonant low end suited to Motörhead's increasingly arena-scaled live shows.

The 2006 configuration used a three rack tom setup (10", 12", 14") and single 16" floor tom, leaner than the four-tom, dual-floor-tom arrangement he'd eventually settle into. The 22" x 18" bass drum, however, was consistent throughout: the same focused, punchy dimension Dee had favored since his earliest Motörhead years.

The birch shell construction gave Dee's drums a full low-mid resonance, audible on album opener "Sucker," where the toms carry sustain and body during fills. Motörhead's stage productions had grown considerably since the mid-1990s club and theater circuit, and the kit's fuller voice suited those larger rooms well.

This was one of the longest-serving stretches of Dee's Sonor SQ2 relationship, spanning the entirety of his later Motörhead career.

### The Steel Snare Behind the Backbeat

Dee's snare choice on *Kiss of Death* carried forward the steel-shell preference he'd maintained since his King Diamond years, voiced through his Sonor SQ2 hardware. At 14" x 6.5", the dimensions matched almost exactly what he'd used on every Motörhead record since *Bastards* — proof that his core sonic priorities remained constant.

The Sonor steel snare delivered a warm, controlled crack, giving Dee's backbeat body without sacrificing the cut needed to sit above Phil Campbell's guitar and Lemmy's aggressive bass tone. On tracks like "Sucker" and "Devil I Know," the snare sits assertively in the mix, driving the tempo with the same crisp authority that had defined his sound for over a decade.

Cameron Webb's returning production kept the snare tuned tight and forward in the mix, consistent with the more modern, defined drum sound he'd established on *Inferno* two years prior.

### Paiste Continuity Through a Gear Transition

On *Kiss of Death*, Dee's cymbal setup remained anchored in the Paiste family he'd played since 1987 — though he began blending the brighter, harder-cutting 2002 series with the warmer, more musical Paiste Signature line. This mirrored a broader trend among rock drummers in the mid-2000s toward Signature's more nuanced voicing for ride and crash work, while retaining 2002 cymbals for hi-hats and heavier accent pieces.

The Signature 22" Full Ride offered more complexity and wash than the straightforward 2002 ride Dee used on *Inferno*, giving tracks like "Living in the Past" and "Christine" — mid-tempo cuts with more room to breathe — a more textured cymbal voice. The 2002 series held its ground for hi-hats, the larger crash, and the China, preserving the cutting projection Dee needed for the album's faster, more aggressive tracks.

This blended setup captures *Kiss of Death* mid-evolution in Dee's cymbal history — a familiar Sonor SQ2 drum voice paired with a cymbal sound that was gradually shifting toward the fuller Signature Series configuration.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What drums has Mikkey Dee played throughout his career?**

A: Mikkey Dee has been a Sonor Drums endorser since his King Diamond days, playing their SQ2 custom-build series. His Sonor SQ2 kit was his setup for [*Kiss of Death*](/articles/kiss-of-death-drum-setup) (2006) and remained his primary kit for nearly the next ten years, refined further across *Motörizer* (2008) and later albums before reaching its final form on [*Bad Magic*](/articles/bad-magic-drum-setup) in 2015. See the [Mikkey Dee drummer profile](/drummer/mikkey-dee) for the complete gear timeline.

**Q: What drum kit did Mikkey Dee use on Kiss of Death?**

A: On *Kiss of Death* (2006), Mikkey Dee played a Sonor SQ2 kit in piano black lacquer finish, with a 22" x 18" bass drum, three rack toms (10", 12", 14"), and a single 16" floor tom. This was a leaner configuration than the four-tom, dual-floor-tom setup he'd later use on [*Bad Magic*](/articles/bad-magic-drum-setup), built with birch shells for fuller low-mid resonance.

**Q: What guest musicians appear on Kiss of Death?**

A: *Kiss of Death* (2006) features several notable guest contributions: Poison guitarist C.C. DeVille added a guitar solo, Alice in Chains bassist Mike Inez contributed additional bass, and Ignite/H2O vocalist Zoltán Téglás provided backing vocals. Despite the outside talent, Mikkey Dee's drumming remained the connective tissue holding the record together — the same economical, powerhouse pocket playing that had defined his Motörhead tenure since his 1993 debut.

**Q: How did Kiss of Death perform commercially?**

A: *Kiss of Death* charted at #4 in Germany — Motörhead's highest German chart position up to that point in the band's career — alongside a UK entry at #45, a Switzerland entry at #26, and a Billboard 200 appearance at #82 in the United States. It was Motörhead's ninth album released through Steamhammer/SPV and the fifteenth studio album of the band's career overall.
