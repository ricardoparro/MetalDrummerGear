# L'Enfant Sauvage Drum Setup: Mario Duplantier's Tama Bubinga Breakthrough

> Discover Mario Duplantier's complete drum kit on Gojira's Grammy-nominated L'Enfant Sauvage. Full breakdown of his Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit, Zildjian cymbals, and the gear behind the title track and 'Explosia'.

## Overview

Released on June 26, 2012, "L'Enfant Sauvage" is the album that transformed Gojira from a cult metal band into a mainstream critical phenomenon. Signed to Roadrunner Records and recorded at Joe Duplantier's Silvercord Studio in Brooklyn, New York, this album arrived with the force of something genuinely new — progressive death metal played with the precision of a Swiss watch and the ferocity of a natural disaster.

At the core of this transformation was Mario Duplantier continuing to refine his longtime Tama and Zildjian setup. Building on the Starclassic Bubinga kit and Zildjian K Custom/A Custom cymbals established on "The Way of All Flesh," Mario dialed in the setup further for this record. The refinement was not cosmetic — it sharpened the tonal character of his playing, introducing a new depth and clarity that suited Gojira's expanding sonic ambitions.

The title track, "L'Enfant Sauvage," earned the band a Grammy nomination for Best Hard Rock/Metal Performance at the 2013 ceremony and landed Gojira on the cover of Guitar World. For a French progressive death metal band, this was extraordinary mainstream recognition — proof that uncompromising heavy music could reach beyond the underground when played with genuine artistry.

Mario's drumming on this album is his most compositionally sophisticated. Where "The Way of All Flesh" demonstrated technical mastery, "L'Enfant Sauvage" reveals a drummer who has internalized his technique so completely that he can think purely in terms of music. The Tama Bubinga shells responded to his touch with the same density and warmth that had defined his sound since "From Mars to Sirius" — and you can hear him exploring the full range of that palette throughout the record.

This article breaks down every component of Mario Duplantier's setup on "L'Enfant Sauvage" and explains why each choice matters to the sound.

### The Refined Foundation: Tama Starclassic Bubinga

Mario Duplantier's Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit for "L'Enfant Sauvage" represented a continued refinement of the setup he had used since "From Mars to Sirius" and "The Way of All Flesh" — the flagship Tama line that became Gojira's breakthrough sound.

Tama Starclassic Bubinga drums are built from dense bubinga and birch hybrid shells, chosen for their warmth and low-end projection in live and studio environments. The bubinga wood provides a dense, warm character with a cleaner, more defined attack — each hit articulates with precision while still blooming into the room.

Mario retained his signature dual bass drum configuration, using two separate 22" x 18" bass drums rather than a double pedal. The Tama 22" kick drums deliver a punchy, focused low end that locks perfectly with Joe Duplantier's down-tuned guitar tones. The 18" depth provides the subsonic presence Gojira demands without sacrificing definition.

The four-tom configuration — two rack toms and two floor toms — remained consistent with his previous albums. Mario's melodic tom approach, using the drums as pitched instruments rather than just fill-fodder, is central to Gojira's sound. The bubinga/birch shells respond with great evenness across the full tuning range, allowing his tribal patterns to sing.

Recording at Silvercord Studio, Joe placed close mics on each drum with room mics capturing the Brooklyn space's character. The bubinga shells' natural warmth gave the drums presence in the mix without requiring significant EQ boost — a key advantage when competing with Gojira's dense, tuned-down guitar arrangements.

### The Strike of the Wild Child

Mario's snare on "L'Enfant Sauvage" is a Tama S.L.P. G-Maple, matched to the rest of his Tama setup. The 14" x 6.5" dimensions are consistent with his long-standing preference — deep enough for authority, wide enough for sensitivity.

The diagonal-grain maple shell produces a warmer, more musical tone than the steel snares favored by many metal drummers. This warmth is essential for Gojira's sound: when Mario's snare cuts through the mix, it should feel like a punch, not a slap. The S.L.P. G-Maple provides exactly this character — dense and purposeful without being clinical.

The drum's tuning rods allow for finer, more consistent tension around the drum head. For a drummer like Mario who demands precise response across a wide dynamic range — from ghost notes in atmospheric passages to full-force rim shots in the heaviest moments — this tuning consistency is genuinely valuable.

On the album's most aggressive tracks, like "Explosia" and the title track, the snare cuts through a mix of down-tuned 7-string guitars and Silvercord Studio's natural room sound. The maple shell's sustain adds body to longer hits without muddying the attack. In quieter passages like "The Wild Healer," the same drum responds to lighter touches with ghost notes that add texture without overpowering the arrangement.

### Dark and Complex: The Zildjian K Custom Setup

Mario Duplantier's continued use of Zildjian K Custom and A Custom cymbals is one of the most consistent aspects of his "L'Enfant Sauvage" gear story. The K Custom series emphasizes darkness and complexity, while the A Custom models add brightness and cut — together forming the hybrid character that defines Gojira's upper-frequency texture.

Zildjian K Custom cymbals are hand-hammered from B20 bronze using a process refined over generations of cymbal craft. The dark, complex tone adds harmonic richness that the brighter A Custom models complement with projection and cut. For 2012-era Gojira, this pairing worked: the album's production has more clarity than previous records, and the cymbals contribute to that open, dynamic sound.

The 14" K Sweet hi-hats provide warm, nuanced articulation for Mario's intricate patterns. Compared to brighter, more aggressive hi-hat options, these hi-hats are precise yet musical — you can hear every stroke clearly even in complex passages.

The crash selection spans 17", 19", and 20", giving Mario a range of decay profiles and volume levels. Zildjian K Custom and A Custom crashes are known for their quick response and musical decay — they don't linger unnaturally, which suits Mario's economical approach to accents.

The 21" Z Custom Mega Bell Ride has a clear, defined bell that Mario exploits for rhythmic accents throughout the record. Unlike smaller, brighter rides, this cymbal maintains clarity and weight when pushed hard in heavy sections.

## The Wild Child Grows Up

"L'Enfant Sauvage" — the wild child — is Gojira's album of maturation without compromise. It is simultaneously their most accessible and most accomplished record: cleaner production, stronger songs, and a drummer who has fully absorbed his influences and transformed them into something entirely personal.

Mario Duplantier's Tama/Zildjian setup on this album was refined rather than replaced. It was a deliberate sonic sharpening — from the dark, organic density of the earlier Tama/Zildjian years to the clear, projected authority of a more articulate Bubinga and K Custom/A Custom voicing. Every element of the refined setup served the new direction: more defined, more confident, more present.

The Grammy nomination validated what Gojira fans already knew: that Mario is among the most complete drummers in modern metal. Not just technically proficient — every aspect of his playing is unified by musical intelligence. He plays for the song, not for the drummer. He plays for the room, not for the gear.

The Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit, Zildjian K Custom/A Custom cymbals, Tama Iron Cobra pedals, and Vic Firth 5B sticks are the tools of a master craftsman at his most deliberate. They do not define his playing; they enable it.

**What "L'Enfant Sauvage" teaches drummers:**
- **Refinement requires commitment**: Sharpening a setup mid-career takes discipline; Mario executed the refinement flawlessly
- **Brightness serves clarity**: Sometimes a more forward-sounding cymbal voicing serves the mix better than a darker, more complex one
- **Endorsements should serve art**: The refined Tama/Zildjian voicing matched Gojira's sonic evolution, not just a business arrangement
- **Grammy recognition follows artistic authenticity**: The title track is Grammy-worthy because it is genuinely great, not because it was designed to be
- **Evolution without abandonment**: Refined gear, same philosophy — tribal grooves, dual kicks, melodic toms

For drummers who want to understand how Mario Duplantier became one of the defining metal drummers of his generation, "L'Enfant Sauvage" is the essential document. It is where the wild child learned that mastery and ferocity are not opposites.

*The wild child never tamed — only refined.*

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What drums did Mario Duplantier use on L'Enfant Sauvage?**

A: On L'Enfant Sauvage (2012), Mario Duplantier played a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit in a Custom Lacquer Black Burst finish. The configuration included dual 22" x 18" bass drums (played as two independent instruments, not a double pedal), 10" and 12" rack toms, and 14" and 16" floor toms. This continued the same Tama Starclassic Bubinga setup used on The Way of All Flesh — the bubinga/birch shells deliver a warm, defined attack suited to the album's more produced sound.

**Q: What cymbals does Mario Duplantier play on L'Enfant Sauvage?**

A: Mario Duplantier continued playing Zildjian K Custom and A Custom cymbals for L'Enfant Sauvage, the same series he had used on earlier Gojira albums. His setup included 14" K Sweet hi-hats, crashes in 17", 19", and 20" sizes, a 21" Z Custom Mega Bell ride, and an 18" K Custom china. The K Custom line's dark, complex character combined with the A Custom's brightness contributes to the album's articulate upper-frequency texture.

**Q: Was L'Enfant Sauvage Grammy nominated?**

A: Yes. Gojira received a Grammy nomination for Best Hard Rock/Metal Performance at the 2013 Grammy Awards for the title track 'L'Enfant Sauvage.' This was a landmark achievement for a French progressive death metal band and confirmed the album's crossover appeal. The band also appeared on the cover of Guitar World magazine during this album cycle — extraordinary mainstream recognition for an uncompromising heavy metal act.
