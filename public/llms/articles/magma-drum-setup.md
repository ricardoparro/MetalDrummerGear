## Overview

Released on June 17, 2016, "Magma" is Gojira's most commercially successful album — debuting at #11 on the US Billboard 200 and reaching #1 in France. It earned the band their first Grammy nomination, for Best Metal Performance ("Silvera") at the 2017 Grammy Awards. For a French progressive death metal band, this level of mainstream recognition was genuinely unprecedented.

At the core of this achievement was Mario Duplantier continuing to refine his longtime Tama and Zildjian setup. Building on the Starclassic Bubinga kit used on "L'Enfant Sauvage," Mario dialed the setup in further for Magma — his most refined configuration to date. The full Zildjian K Custom/A Custom hybrid cymbal setup, paired with the dense bubinga/birch shells, delivers a warm yet focused character that suited the album's leaner, more direct production approach.

Where "L'Enfant Sauvage" was dense and complex, Magma stripped Gojira's sound to its essential elements. The production is cleaner and more concise — songs are shorter, arrangements are more direct, and every drum part carries greater structural weight. Mario's Tama setup responded perfectly to this philosophy: warm, punchy, defined.

The Grammy nomination for "Silvera" confirmed what fans already knew: that Gojira had produced their most universally accessible record without compromising the intensity and originality that made them essential. Mario's drumming on Magma is his most song-focused and rhythmically driven — technique completely in service of the track.

This article breaks down every component of Mario Duplantier's setup on Magma and explains why each choice shapes the sound.

### The Tama Starclassic Bubinga: Bubinga/Birch Refinement

The Tama Starclassic Bubinga is the refinement Mario Duplantier needed for Magma's leaner, more direct production approach. Building on the same kit used on "L'Enfant Sauvage," the bubinga/birch hybrid construction delivers a distinctive character: the bubinga provides density and low-end warmth, while the birch layer adds focused midrange attack. The result is a shell that is simultaneously punchy and musical — dense without becoming muddy.

For Gojira's 2016 sound, this tonal balance was decisive. Magma is the most streamlined production in the band's catalog — fewer layers, more space, greater dynamic contrast. The Bubinga's character sits in the mix with presence and warmth without excess brightness. In down-tuned, guitar-dense arrangements, this translates to drums that cut through without competing.

Mario retained his signature dual bass drum configuration, using two independent 22" x 18" Tama Starclassic Bubinga kick drums. Tama's 22" bass drums deliver the focused, punchy low end that defines Gojira's rhythmic identity. The Bubinga kick punches with clarity — the attack is immediate and the sustain controlled, exactly what Joe Duplantier's Silver Cord Studio production demands.

The four-tom configuration — two rack toms and two floor toms — remained Mario's constant across every album. His melodic approach to toms, treating them as pitched extensions of the composition rather than fill vessels, demands consistent shell behavior across the full frequency range. The bubinga/birch hybrid responds evenly at any tuning, from tight high-tension rack toms to deep, resonant floor toms.

### The Strike of Silvera

Mario's snare on Magma is a Tama S.L.P. G-Maple, matched to the rest of the kit. The 14" x 6.5" dimensions reflect his long-standing preference — deep enough for authority, wide enough for sensitivity at lower dynamics.

The diagonal-grain maple shell produces a warmer, more complex tone than a straight-maple or steel snare. This character is audible throughout Magma: the snare sits in the mix with body and purpose, not as a sharp, clinical strike. On "Silvera" and "Stranded," the snare's warmth fills the space without overwhelming Joe's minimal guitar arrangements.

The G-Maple's diagonal-grain construction, combined with the shell's natural resonance, provides even response across the drum head. For Mario's wide dynamic range — from delicate ghost notes in atmospheric sections to punishing rim shots in the heaviest passages — this consistency is essential. The same drum that whispers in a verse has to thunder in a chorus.

On Magma's most intense tracks, the snare carries structural weight that would crush a lighter instrument. The 6.5" depth provides the body for power while the maple responds quickly enough for the intricate patterns Mario employs in mid-tempo grooves.

### Full Zildjian Hybrid: K Custom / A Custom Setup

Mario Duplantier's full Zildjian K Custom/A Custom hybrid cymbal setup for Magma represents a continued deliberate tonal refinement. The K Custom line offers darkness and complexity while the A Custom models deliver brightness and cut — together forming a fullness and musicality that aligns with the Starclassic Bubinga drums' character.

Zildjian K Custom and A Custom cymbals are hand-hammered from B20 bronze to Zildjian's exacting standards. The combined series is known for its balanced, full-bodied tone: present and projecting without brightness becoming harshness. For Magma's production, which strips away density and favors space, these cymbals provide exactly the right upper-frequency character.

The 14" K Sweet hi-hats produce a controlled, musical sound with enough weight for Mario's driving patterns. They articulate clearly without the sharp edge of an overly bright hi-hat — each stroke is defined but warm. On tracks like "Stranded," the hi-hats drive the groove with authority that complements the direct, riff-based arrangement.

The crash selection — 17", 19", and 20" — gives Mario a full range of response times and sustain profiles. The 17" A Custom Crash decays quickly for sharp accents without lingering; the 20" K Custom Dark Crash sustains for musical resolution of longer phrases. Zildjian K/A Custom crashes are known for their musical character — they sit in a mix rather than dominating it.

The 21" Z Custom Mega Bell Ride provides the powerful bell accents that Mario uses for rhythmic punctuation throughout the album. Unlike smaller ride cymbals, the Mega Bell can be crashed without losing definition, giving it flexibility in Gojira's dynamic arrangements.

## The Album That Introduced Gojira to the World

Magma is Gojira's most influential record. Not necessarily their most complex — "From Mars to Sirius" has greater compositional ambition. Not their most technically demanding — "The Way of All Flesh" pushes further. But Magma is the album that proved Gojira could be both uncompromisingly heavy and universally accessible simultaneously.

Mario Duplantier's Tama Starclassic Bubinga setup on this album was not a compromise. It was a refinement. The bubinga/birch shell, the Zildjian K Custom/A Custom hybrid cymbals, the Tama Iron Cobra pedals — every choice served the album's stripped-back philosophy. The drums on Magma have weight, warmth, and space. In Joe Duplantier's direct production, they sit in the mix like something inevitable.

The Grammy nomination for "Silvera" was the official recognition. But the real proof is in the drum fills of "The Shooting Star" — written for Mario's late mother — where a drummer who could play anything chooses to play almost nothing. That restraint, that musical intelligence, that understanding of when silence serves better than sound, is what makes Mario Duplantier exceptional.

**What Magma teaches drummers:**
- **Warmth serves directness**: The Starclassic Bubinga's bubinga/birch character suited Magma's leaner production better than a brighter voicing
- **Gear refinement follows musical direction**: Every tuning and voicing adjustment aligned with Gojira's sonic evolution
- **Restraint is the highest technique**: The most moving moments on Magma involve less, not more
- **Grammy recognition comes from authenticity**: "Silvera" earned its nomination by being genuinely great, not designed for commercial appeal
- **Consistency of philosophy across gear refinements**: Dual kicks, four toms, tribal patterns — the approach remained constant

The [L'Enfant Sauvage drum setup](/articles/lenfant-sauvage-drum-setup) introduced the brighter, more produced Tama/Zildjian voicing. Magma refined it into something more essential. For drummers who want to understand how Mario Duplantier became the most influential progressive metal drummer of his generation, both records are required listening — and Magma is where the argument becomes undeniable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What drums did Mario Duplantier use on Magma?**

A: On Magma (2016), Mario Duplantier played a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit. The bubinga/birch hybrid shell construction delivers a warm yet focused tone, refined further from the voicing used on L'Enfant Sauvage into a warmer, more focused balance for Magma's leaner production. His configuration included dual 22" x 18" bass drums (two independent instruments, not a double pedal), 10" and 12" rack toms, and 14" and 16" floor toms. He paired the Tama kit with Tama Iron Cobra 900 single pedals — one for each bass drum.

**Q: Was Magma Grammy nominated?**

A: Yes. Gojira received their first-ever Grammy nomination for Best Metal Performance at the 2017 Grammy Awards (59th Grammy Awards) for "Silvera" from Magma. This was a landmark achievement — Gojira became the first French metal band to receive a Grammy nomination in any category. The nomination reflected Magma's commercial and critical breakthrough: the album debuted at #11 on the US Billboard 200 and reached #1 in France, making it the highest-charting Gojira release to date. Gojira was nominated alongside Baroness, Disturbed, Periphery, and Trivium, ultimately losing to Disturbed.

**Q: How does Magma's production differ from L'Enfant Sauvage?**

A: Magma is significantly leaner and more direct than L'Enfant Sauvage. Where L'Enfant Sauvage featured dense arrangements, complex guitar layering, and a produced clarity from a brighter Tama Bubinga/Zildjian voicing, Magma strips Gojira's sound to its essential elements. Songs are shorter, arrangements are sparser, and the production foregrounds the core guitar-bass-drums interaction. Mario's further refinement of the Tama Starclassic Bubinga tuning contributed to this: the bubinga/birch hybrid shells add warmth and body that suits the album's more minimal production. The Zildjian K Custom/A Custom hybrid cymbal setup also shifted the upper-frequency character — from the brighter L'Enfant Sauvage-era voicing to a fuller, more musical tone that fits the album's direct approach. The result is Gojira's most accessible album without sacrificing the intensity and originality that defines them.

**Q: What cymbals did Mario Duplantier use on Magma?**

A: For Magma, Mario Duplantier played a full Zildjian K Custom/A Custom hybrid cymbal setup. His setup included 14" K Sweet hi-hats, crashes at 17", 19", and 20", a 21" Z Custom Mega Bell Ride, and an 18" K Custom China. The K Custom and A Custom lines are hand-hammered from B20 bronze and deliver a full, musical tone with controlled warmth. Compared to the brighter voicing used on L'Enfant Sauvage, this Magma-era hybrid setup sits warmer in the mix — a tonal complement to the Tama Starclassic Bubinga drums' bubinga/birch character.
