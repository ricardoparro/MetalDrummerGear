# Mikkey Dee Gear Evolution — Motörhead & Scorpions Drum Kit History

> From King Diamond's theatrical metal to 23 unbroken years behind Lemmy's band: the complete history of Mikkey Dee's drum gear evolution across King Diamond, Motörhead, and Scorpions.

**Type:** Gear Evolution Timeline
**Drummer(s):** [Mikkey Dee](/llms/drummers/mikkey-dee.md)
**Band / Album:** Motörhead
**Genre:** Heavy Metal / Hard Rock

## Overview

Mikkey Dee holds one of the longest unbroken tenures in metal drumming history: 23 years as Motörhead's drummer, from Bastards (1993) to Lemmy Kilmister's death in December 2015. In that time he appeared on eleven studio albums, toured relentlessly across every major market, and became the sonic engine behind what many consider the definitive version of Motörhead's classic lineup.

His gear evolution spans three phases: a King Diamond apprenticeship that built professional discipline with no fixed drum brand, a long Motörhead era on Tama drums that reached its climax with the Grammy-winning Bad Magic (2015) after a 2012 switch to Sonor, and an ongoing Scorpions chapter that continues today. Paiste cymbals, unbroken since 1987, and Evans heads for the final decade — the progression is steady rather than dramatic, reflecting a drummer who found what worked and refined rather than reinvented.

What makes Dee's evolution significant is not radical change but extraordinary consistency maintained across four decades of professional touring. Every upgrade was driven by the demands of an ever-larger stage, not by fashion or endorsement convenience.

## Era 1: King Diamond Era (1985–1990)

**Kit:** Various kits, no single fixed brand documented (22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms)
**Snare:** Stock birch snare 14"×5.5"
**Cymbals:** Paiste Signature Series (14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride)
**Pedals:** Standard cam-driven double pedal
**Heads:** Remo Ambassador

Mikkey Dee joined King Diamond's band in 1985, recording Fatal Portrait (1986) and Abigail (1987) before a brief stint with Don Dokken's band in 1988, then returning as a session player on Conspiracy (1989) and The Eye (1990). The King Diamond years were his professional apprenticeship: technically demanding melodic metal with theatrical staging requirements that taught him the discipline of precise performance within complex arrangements.

Dee became a Paiste artist in 1987 — bright, cutting, durable cymbals that provided the projecting character needed to cut through King Diamond's elaborate stage productions, and the one constant that carried through everything that followed. No fixed drum brand endorsement existed yet during this period.

Four albums and multiple world tours in five years gave Dee the professional foundation that made his Motörhead tenure possible.

*Albums:* Fatal Portrait (1986) · Abigail (1987) · Conspiracy (1989) · The Eye (1990)

## Era 2: Motörhead Era — Tama to Sonor (1993–2015)

**Kit:** Tama Artstar II / Swingstar, 1993–2012 (22"×18" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms); Sonor SQ2, 2012–2015 (maple/birch hybrid, 22"×18" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms)
**Snare:** Tama steel 14"×6.5" (1993–2012); Sonor SQ2 Mikkey Dee Signature Snare, 14"×7.25" birch (2012–2015)
**Cymbals:** Paiste Signature Series — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride, expanded to 17"/18" crashes, 21" ride, 19" China from 2012
**Pedals:** DW 5000 Double Pedal
**Sticks:** Wincent 5B, later Wincent Mikkey Dee Signature (from 2012)
**Heads:** Remo Emperor (toms) / Powerstroke 3 (kick), 1993–2004; Evans G2 (toms) / EMAD (kick), 2004–2015

Mikkey Dee joined Motörhead in 1992, replacing Phil Taylor ahead of Bastards (1993, ZYX Music). He joined on a Tama Artstar II/Swingstar kit, and the combination of Tama's punchy character and the band's massive touring schedule drove consistent reliability across more than a decade. His Paiste cymbals, already in place since 1987, carried over unchanged.

The Tama steel snare's deep, walloping crack became Dee's signature sound across Bastards, Sacrifice (1995), Overnight Sensation (1996), Snake Bite Love (1998), and We Are Motörhead (2000). Motörhead's touring schedule during this period was among the most relentless in rock — up to 200 shows per year — demanding hardware that could withstand extraordinary abuse. Tama delivered, and DW 5000 pedals carried the load underfoot throughout.

In 2012, with Kiss of Death (2006), Motörizer (2008), The Wörld Is Yours (2010), and Aftershock (2013) behind him and Motörhead's stages larger than ever, Dee signed with Sonor, switching to a Sonor SQ2 kit and releasing a Mikkey Dee Signature Snare as part of the deal. Evans heads — G2 on toms, EMAD on kick — had already replaced Remo in 2004 for warmer resonance and focused low-end at arena volumes. The Wincent Mikkey Dee signature sticks reflected his consolidated status as one of hard rock's most recognised drummers.

Bad Magic (2015) won a Grammy Award for Best Metal Performance for "Thunder & Lightning" — Motörhead's first and only Grammy. Lemmy Kilmister died on December 28, 2015, 23 days after receiving his cancer diagnosis. Motörhead disbanded. Dee's 23-year tenure — the longest in the band's history — ended.

*Albums:* [Bastards (1993) drum setup](/articles/bastards-drum-setup) · We Are Motörhead (2000) · [Kiss of Death (2006) drum setup](/articles/kiss-of-death-drum-setup) · [Bad Magic (2015) drum setup](/articles/bad-magic-drum-setup)

## Era 3: Scorpions Era (2016–Present)

**Kit:** Sonor SQ2 (continued) — maple/birch hybrid, 22"×18" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms
**Snare:** Sonor SQ2 Mikkey Dee Signature Snare, 14"×7.25" birch
**Cymbals:** Paiste Signature Series (continued) — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" crashes, 21" ride, 19" China
**Pedals:** DW 5000 Series Double Pedal (continued)
**Heads:** Evans G2 (toms), Evans EMAD2 (kick)

Mikkey Dee joined Scorpions in 2016, appearing on Rock Believer (2022) — his first full studio album with the band recorded from the start. The move from Motörhead's three-chord thunder to Scorpions' broader melodic hard rock dynamic required adjustments in approach rather than wholesale gear change.

His Sonor SQ2 and Paiste Signature endorsements, both carried over from Motörhead's final years, remain unchanged. EMAD2 kick heads replace EMAD for tighter, more refined low-end suited to Scorpions' polished arena production values.

The Sonor SQ2 platform that served him through Motörhead's final years continues unchanged — a testimonial to a setup refined over the most demanding touring in hard rock.

*Albums:* Rock Believer (2022) · [Bad Magic drum setup](/articles/bad-magic-drum-setup) (final Motörhead album, informing current era)

## FAQ

**Q: What drum kit did Mikkey Dee use with Motörhead?**
Mikkey Dee played Tama Artstar II/Swingstar drums from his 1992 arrival through 2012 — a 22"×18" kick with 10"/12"/14"/16" toms, first heard on Bastards (1993). In 2012 he signed with Sonor and switched to a Sonor SQ2 kit with a Mikkey Dee Signature Snare, the setup he used through Motörhead's final album, Bad Magic (2015). That era encompasses Kiss of Death (2006), Motörizer (2008), The Wörld Is Yours (2010), Aftershock (2013), and Bad Magic (2015).

**Q: What snare drum does Mikkey Dee use?**
Mikkey Dee played a Tama steel snare in 14"×6.5" throughout the early Motörhead years, driving the walloping snare tone across the band's mid-career albums. Since 2012 his primary snare has been the Sonor SQ2 Mikkey Dee Signature Snare, 14"×7.25" birch — a configuration he has carried through Bad Magic and into his current Scorpions tenure.

**Q: What cymbals does Mikkey Dee use?**
Mikkey Dee has played Paiste Signature Series cymbals throughout his entire career — an endorsement unbroken since 1987, through King Diamond, the full Motörhead era, and his current Scorpions tenure. The configuration expanded from a King Diamond-era 14"/16"/18"/20" setup to a broader 14"/17"/18"/21"/19" configuration from 2012 onward to fill larger arena and festival stages.

**Q: Did Motörhead ever win a Grammy?**
Yes — Bad Magic (2015) won the Grammy Award for Best Metal Performance for the track "Thunder & Lightning." It was Motörhead's first and only Grammy. Lemmy Kilmister died on December 28, 2015, just weeks after the award was announced. Mikkey Dee was the drummer on Bad Magic — making him part of the band's only Grammy-winning recording in their entire 40-year career.

**Q: What band is Mikkey Dee in now?**
Since Motörhead's dissolution following Lemmy Kilmister's death in December 2015, Mikkey Dee has been the drummer for Scorpions. He joined in 2016 and appeared on Rock Believer (2022) — his first full studio album with the band. He continues to tour with Scorpions on their ongoing world tour schedule.
