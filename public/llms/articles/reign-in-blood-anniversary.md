# Reign in Blood at 40: Dave Lombardo's Gear, 40 Years Later

## Overview

Slayer released Reign in Blood in October 1986 through Def Jam Recordings — 29 minutes that became the template for extreme metal drumming. The exact release day is disputed: Slayer's own 30th-anniversary social post, Metal Archives, and Discogs all list October 7, while Wikipedia and Last.fm list October 20. The gap traces back to 1986 itself — Columbia/CBS refused to distribute the album over the content of its opening track, "Angel of Death," and Def Jam had to scramble an alternate distribution deal through Geffen, muddying exactly when the record actually reached stores. Forty years on, MetalForge marks the anniversary without pretending that ambiguity has been resolved.

What isn't disputed is what's on the record. Dave Lombardo drums on every track, "Angel of Death" opens the album with the double-bass assault that still defines the genre, and Rick Rubin produced the sessions with a stripped-down, no-reverb approach that let Lombardo's playing carry the record. This piece revisits that gear and performance through the lens of four decades of influence.

## The Speed That Redefined Metal

Lombardo recorded Reign in Blood on a Tama Artstar II kit in Piano Black, running two 22" bass drums independently rather than a single kick with a double pedal — a choice that gave every stroke identical attack at tempos other drummers couldn't sustain with one foot. His Tama Superstar Steel snare, tuned high and tight, cut through the record's dry, close-mic'd production, and Paiste's RUDE and 2002 series cymbals supplied the crashes and Chinas that punctuate the riffs on "Angel of Death" and "Raining Blood."

None of it would matter without the playing. "Angel of Death" sustains blast beats past 200 BPM for minutes at a stretch, "Criminally Insane" packs relentless speed into under three minutes, and the transition from "Postmortem" into "Raining Blood" is still one of metal's most-cited drum performances. Forty years later, that combination of raw velocity and Cuban-influenced groove is still the reason drummers study this record rather than just headbang to it.

- Tama Artstar II kit, Piano Black finish, twin independent 22" bass drums
- Tama Superstar Steel 14" x 6.5" snare, tuned high for maximum cut
- Paiste RUDE & 2002 series cymbals for crashes, hi-hats, and Chinas
- "Angel of Death" sustains 200+ BPM blast beats across the album opener

## 40 Years of Influence

Reign in Blood's drum chair has a direct line of succession within Slayer itself: Paul Bostaph took over on drums for the band's early-90s and 2000s stretches, building his own reputation on the foundation Lombardo poured on this record — the two are compared directly in our Paul Bostaph vs Dave Lombardo breakdown. Beyond Slayer, Lombardo's playing on this album is routinely cited as a founding document of extreme metal drumming, referenced by death metal and grindcore drummers who came up learning "Angel of Death" and "Raining Blood" note for note.

Four decades after release, Reign in Blood still functions as a technical benchmark rather than a nostalgia piece — the gear is still largely in production, the tracks are still played live, and the drumming is still what extreme metal drummers get measured against.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: When was Slayer's Reign in Blood released?**

A: Reign in Blood was released in October 1986 through Def Jam Recordings, but the exact day is disputed. Slayer's own 30th-anniversary post, Metal Archives, and Discogs list October 7, while Wikipedia and Last.fm list October 20. Columbia/CBS's refusal to distribute the album over "Angel of Death" forced a scramble for alternate distribution, which is the likely source of the date confusion.

**Q: Why is Reign in Blood's release date disputed?**

A: Columbia/CBS, Def Jam's distributor at the time, refused to distribute Reign in Blood because of the content of its opening track, "Angel of Death." Def Jam had to arrange alternate distribution through Geffen, and that scramble is why sources disagree on the exact release date — some list October 7, 1986, others October 20, 1986.

**Q: Who played drums on Reign in Blood?**

A: Dave Lombardo played drums on every track of Reign in Blood. His performance — sustained blast beats, precise double bass, and Cuban-influenced fills — remains one of the most studied drum performances in thrash metal.

**Q: What drum gear did Dave Lombardo use on Reign in Blood?**

A: Lombardo recorded Reign in Blood on a Tama Artstar II kit (Piano Black finish) with twin independent 22" bass drums, a Tama Superstar Steel 14" x 6.5" snare, and Paiste RUDE and 2002 series cymbals. The full track-by-track breakdown, including hardware and recording technique, is in our Reign in Blood drum setup article.

**Q: Is Dave Lombardo's Reign in Blood-era gear still available?**

A: The Paiste RUDE and 2002 series cymbals he used remain in production largely unchanged. The Tama Artstar II itself is a discontinued vintage model found through Reverb and used-gear dealers; Lombardo's current signature ProMark TX2BXN sticks are a separate, present-day product.
