## Overview

Travis Orbin (born July 5, 1985, in Baltimore, Maryland) played drums in Periphery from around August/September 2006 to February 2009, in the band's pre-recording years before Misha Mansoor's project settled into the lineup that would write and record its self-titled debut. Orbin left before that album — released March 30, 2010 — was written or recorded, and he does not perform on it, on Periphery II (2012), or on any Periphery release. Matt Halpern joined as his replacement in 2009 and has recorded every Periphery album since; see [Matt Halpern's complete gear guide at MetalForge](/drummer/matt-halpern) for that era.

Despite never appearing on a Periphery record, Orbin built a reputation among technical drummers independent of the band. His polyrhythmic architecture, structural ghost-note density, and independent two-footed bass drum work — documented extensively in his own solo and cover videos — made him a reference point for progressive metal drummers on the strength of that work rather than a studio credit.

After leaving Periphery, Orbin toured with Darkest Hour and recorded with Sky Harbor, building a career as a sought-after technical session player. His YouTube drum playthroughs — detailed, musically informed breakdowns of complex drum parts — have garnered millions of views and established him as one of drumming's most respected educators.

Travis Orbin's SJC Custom kit, Zildjian cymbals, Roland SPD-SX integration, and trigger configuration are the mechanical foundation of a drumming approach built to handle extreme technical density without sacrificing groove.

### SJC Custom: Flagship Construction for Elite Djent

Travis Orbin's SJC Custom kit is built to the specifications that professional drummers designing their ideal acoustic instrument demand. SJC's custom shop line represents shells built to order — precisely specified ply counts, bearing edge profiles, and finish combinations that affect every acoustic parameter of the drum.

For Orbin's djent application, the SJC Custom North American maple shells provide the combination of midrange warmth and high-frequency attack that modern progressive metal requires. Maple's natural brightness preserves the articulation of complex polyrhythmic patterns — ghost notes, linear passages, rapid bass drum sequences — without allowing individual strokes to blur in dense mixes. SJC's custom bearing edges further refine this by controlling sustain exactly, allowing Orbin to tune for the focused, punchy tone that djent production demands.

The double bass configuration — two 22" x 18" kick drums — provides the physical and acoustic symmetry that two-footed djent patterns require. Both drums are treated identically: same head selection, same tuning, same dampening configuration, ensuring each foot produces the same attack and decay. At the tempos and pattern densities Orbin's music demands, consistency between kicks is not aesthetic preference — it's structural necessity.

His four-tom configuration (10", 12", 14", 16") covers the melodic range that Periphery's compositions require without overcomplicating the kit layout. Orbin's fills function as melodic countermelodies in arrangements of considerable harmonic sophistication — these toms need to speak at distinct pitches, and the SJC Custom shell precision makes melodic tuning reliable.

The kit's visual presentation adapts to each recording or touring context, but the acoustic signature remains constant: controlled, articulate, precise — the perfect acoustic platform for layering electronic triggers and samples.

### The Snare: Crack and Sensitivity for Polyrhythmic Work

Travis Orbin's snare choice prioritizes a combination that his playing style demands above all else: projection and sensitivity coexisting in the same instrument. His 14" x 6.5" SJC Custom maple snare delivers the crack that cuts through Periphery's dense guitar arrangements while maintaining the ghost note sensitivity essential to his linear drumming vocabulary.

The 6.5" depth provides additional body compared to standard 5.5" models without crossing into the territory where extended sustain becomes a problem in fast djent patterns. At the tempos and ghost note densities Orbin plays, a snare that rings too long creates sonic clutter; one that cuts off too sharply loses musical body. The SJC Custom 6.5" maple model sits precisely in the middle range.

Orbin's ghost note technique is one of the defining elements of his Periphery-era playing. Complex linear patterns that appear as single-voice passages are actually multi-voice textures built from precisely calibrated ghost notes. The SJC Custom snare's dynamic sensitivity — its ability to register near-silent touches and translate them into musically present but not dominant tones — makes these patterns possible acoustically. Triggering and processing complete the picture in recorded contexts, but the acoustic foundation must be right first.

### Zildjian: Tonal Versatility for Djent Architecture

Travis Orbin's Zildjian setup reflects the dual demands of djent drumming: the hi-hats must produce articulate, clearly defined strokes at very high tempos and pattern densities, while the crash and ride cymbals need dynamic range from whisper-quiet to explosive to serve Periphery's compositional contrast.

The 14" K Custom Dark Hi-Hats are the foundation. The K Custom Dark series produces a complex, layered tone with a lower fundamental than bright alternatives — giving Orbin's hi-hat patterns a character that sits naturally in djent's mid-frequency guitar landscape without competing for the same sonic space. Fast single-stroke patterns and complex open-closed sequences read clearly through dense guitar arrangements because the K Custom Dark hi-hats speak with definition without harshness.

His crash configuration spans two A Custom models. The A Custom series produces a bright, fast-responding, washy crash voice that speaks immediately on impact — critical when Orbin is accenting within fast-moving djent patterns where crash decay must resolve quickly before the next rhythmic event. The 18" provides the primary, faster-decaying voice; the 19" covers larger dynamic accents where more wash serves the arrangement.

The 21" K Custom Dark Ride provides controlled sustain with enough bell presence to be heard in heavier passages. Unlike brighter rides that can create excessive wash in dense mixes, the K Custom Dark keeps the ride functional in Periphery's guitar-dense context — a clear ping for riding patterns, controlled wash that doesn't build against itself.

The 18" A Custom China delivers the sharp, upward-projecting accent that djent's rhythmic punctuation requires. Quick attack, fast decay, immediate and then gone — the China's role in Orbin's architecture is rhythmic exclamation rather than sustained color.

## A Technically Demanding Voice Beyond the Studio Discography

Travis Orbin's reputation among technical drummers rests almost entirely on work outside any Periphery studio credit. He played in Periphery from 2006 to 2009 — the band's pre-recording years — then built his name through session work, touring with Darkest Hour and Sky Harbor, and, most visibly, through YouTube playthrough videos breaking down extreme technical drumming for a wide audience.

His SJC Custom kit, Zildjian K Custom and A Custom cymbals, Roland SPD-SX electronic integration, and trigger system are not just gear choices — they are an integrated design philosophy. Every element is selected to support the technical demands of his playing: acoustic precision as the foundation, electronic consistency as the layer that makes complex patterns reliable in production contexts, and tonal choices calibrated for a dense, high-gain frequency environment.

**What Travis Orbin's Setup Teaches:**

- **The hybrid approach is intentional**: Electronic triggers and the Roland SPD-SX are compositional tools he uses across his session and solo work, not post-production fixes.

- **Acoustic quality is non-negotiable**: SJC's custom shop line is not a budget compromise. Professional electronic integration requires professional acoustic foundations — the trigger must capture a genuine acoustic stroke, not mask a mediocre one.

- **Polyrhythmic playing demands gear that can keep up**: Orbin's Zildjian K Custom Dark Hi-Hats provide the articulate, fast-responding tone his polyrhythmic hi-hat patterns require. Bright, washy cymbals would blur the complex patterns he plays; the K Custom Dark keeps each stroke distinct.

- **Ghost notes require acoustic sensitivity**: The SJC Custom snare's dynamic range — its ability to register ghost notes at near-silent velocities and translate them faithfully — is what makes his linear and polyrhythmic patterns possible.

**The Periphery Connection:**

Orbin's Periphery tenure (2006–2009) predates the band's recorded output entirely; Matt Halpern joined in 2009 and has been the drummer on every Periphery release since, starting with the 2010 self-titled debut. The two are not sequential eras of the same discography — Orbin's Periphery years and Halpern's studio catalog don't overlap.

**Q: How does Travis Orbin get his djent drum sound?**

A: Travis Orbin achieves his djent drum sound through a hybrid acoustic-electronic approach. Acoustically, his SJC Custom maple shells and Evans EMAD bass drum heads produce a controlled, focused attack with minimal unwanted sustain. Electronic triggers on the bass drums feed the Roland SPD-SX sampling pad system, adding consistent sample layering that reinforces and extends the acoustic kick sound. This trigger integration — fundamental to modern djent drum production — ensures that complex two-footed patterns register with equal attack regardless of physical variation in playing dynamics. The Roland SPD-SX also allows Orbin to introduce electronic textures and samples as compositional elements within the live and studio setup. The result is a drum sound that maintains acoustic warmth and dynamic sensitivity while delivering the attack precision and consistency that djent production demands.

**Q: How does Travis Orbin use electronics and triggers in his drum setup?**

A: Travis Orbin integrates electronics through two primary systems: bass drum triggers and the Roland SPD-SX sampling pad. Bass drum triggers — mounted on the acoustic kick drum batter heads — convert each physical stroke into a consistent electronic signal that can be processed or sample-layered, eliminating the attack variability inherent in acoustic-only recording at djent tempos. The Roland SPD-SX extends the kit's sonic vocabulary beyond acoustics: electronic textures, sample layers, and processed sounds are triggered from the pad as compositional elements of the drum arrangement rather than as supplemental effects. This hybrid philosophy means Orbin's kit operates simultaneously as an acoustic instrument and an electronic one, with each element designed to serve the overall sound rather than occupy separate roles.
