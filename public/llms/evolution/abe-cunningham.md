# Abe Cunningham Drum Kit Evolution — Complete Timeline

Abe Cunningham is the founding drummer of the Deftones, one of alternative metal's most critically acclaimed and influential bands. Co-founding the Sacramento group in 1988 alongside Chino Moreno, Stephen Carpenter, and the late Chi Cheng, Cunningham has been the rhythmic backbone of every Deftones record across nearly four decades. His evolution spans hardcore-rooted aggression, the atmospheric breakthroughs of White Pony, the groove sophistication of Diamond Eyes, and the cinematic density of Gore and Ohms — a career arc as rich as any in modern heavy music. This timeline documents his complete gear evolution from ddrum beginnings through his 1997 Tama signing to the present-day Zildjian cymbal return.

## Founding / Early Deftones Era (1988–1994)

**Albums:** Adrenaline (1995) — recorded during this period
**Tours:** Sacramento club circuit, early California DIY touring

Abe Cunningham co-founded the Deftones while still in high school in Sacramento. The early years were shaped by hardcore punk and alternative metal — raw, hard-hitting drumming on budget gear held together by teenage energy and relentless local gigging. His ddrum kit became the foundation of his sound before the band signed to Maverick Records in 1994.

- **Drums:** ddrum Dios Series — maple shells: 22" kick, 12"/13"/16" toms (durable, punchy option favored by hard-hitting alternative metal drummers in the early 1990s)
- **Snare:** ddrum Steel 14"×5.5" (bright, cutting steel for hardcore-influenced attack)
- **Cymbals:** Zildjian A Series — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride (reliable, bright A Series across early gigging years)
- **Hardware:** Various budget hardware — single kick configuration
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth 5B (heavy hickory for aggressive playing)
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador clear on toms, coated on snare
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$2,500

**Key developments:**
- Deftones co-founded in Sacramento, California (1988)
- Hardcore and alternative metal roots establish aggressive drumming foundation
- ddrum Dios kit becomes Cunningham's early signature brand
- Signed to Maverick Records (1994) — major label breakthrough

## Adrenaline / Around the Fur Era (1995–1999)

**Albums:** Adrenaline (1995), Around the Fur (1997)
**Tours:** Adrenaline Tour 1995–1996, Around the Fur Tour 1997–1999, Warped Tour appearances, Ozzfest 1998

**Q: What drums did Abe Cunningham use on Adrenaline and Around the Fur?**
A: On Adrenaline (1995), Cunningham was still playing the ddrum Dios Series kit from the Deftones' early years. He signed with Tama, Zildjian, and Pro-Mark in 1997, debuting the Tama Starclassic kit — paired with Tama Iron Cobra hardware and a Pro-Mark stick endorsement — on Around the Fur, relationships that have lasted his entire career. His Zildjian A Custom cymbals replaced the earlier A Series for a brighter, more cutting sound suited to the Ozzfest stage.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic — maple shells: 22" kick, 10"/12"/13"/16" toms *(switch from ddrum — signed with Tama in 1997, debuting the Starclassic on Around the Fur)*
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic 14"×6.5" *(switch — deeper maple shell for more crack and weight)*
- **Cymbals:** Zildjian A Custom Series — 14" A Custom hi-hats, 16"/18" A Custom crashes, 21" A Custom ride *(switch — signed with Zildjian in 1997; brighter, more cutting for nu-metal production and Ozzfest stages)*
- **Hardware:** Tama Iron Cobra 900 Rolling Glide *(switch — double kick added for Around the Fur's heavier material)*
- **Sticks:** Pro-Mark *(switch — signed with Pro-Mark in 1997, later formalized into his signature TX916W model)*
- **Heads:** Remo Emperor on toms *(upgrade — durability for constant Ozzfest and Warped Tour touring)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$6,000

**Key developments:**
- Adrenaline (1995) — major label debut breaks Deftones into mainstream; recorded on ddrum
- Signed with Tama in 1997, debuting the Starclassic kit on Around the Fur — a relationship that has lasted his entire career
- Around the Fur (1997) — nu-metal breakthrough; "My Own Summer" and "Be Quiet and Drive" become anthems
- Double kick configuration added via Tama Iron Cobra hardware
- Ozzfest 1998 — massive audience exposure
- Groove-forward style distinguishes Cunningham from heavier nu-metal peers

## White Pony Era (2000–2003)

**Albums:** White Pony (2000)
**Tours:** White Pony World Tour 2000–2002

**Q: What drums did Abe Cunningham use on White Pony?**
A: For White Pony (2000), Cunningham stayed on the Tama Starclassic kit, Tama Iron Cobra hardware, and Pro-Mark sticks he'd signed in 1997 — no gear brand change accompanied the album's atmospheric arrangements, which move between shoegaze, post-rock, and heavy metal within a single album. His cymbal palette shifted to Zildjian K Series — darker, more complex Turkish bronze that suited the album's textured, atmospheric sound.

White Pony won the Grammy Award for Best Metal Performance ("Elite") and is widely considered one of the greatest alternative metal albums ever made. Cunningham's dynamic, spacious drumming was central to its success.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic — maple shells: 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms *(continued — same kit signed in 1997, no brand change for White Pony's atmospheric arrangements)*
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic Brass 14"×6.5" *(upgrade — warm brass shell for the dynamic range from quiet to crushing across White Pony)*
- **Cymbals:** Zildjian K Series — 14" K hi-hats, 16"/18" K crashes, 20" K ride *(switch from A Custom — darker, more complex Turkish bronze for White Pony's textured sound)*
- **Hardware:** Tama Iron Cobra 900 Rolling Glide *(continued)*
- **Sticks:** Pro-Mark *(continued)*
- **Heads:** Remo Emperor on toms, Coated Ambassador on snare *(upgrade — coated snare for warmer, more controlled attack)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$8,000

**Key developments:**
- White Pony (2000) — Grammy Award winner; career-defining masterpiece
- Continued on the Tama Starclassic signed in 1997 — no drum brand change for White Pony
- Switched to Zildjian K — darker, more atmospheric cymbal voice
- Atmospheric, dynamic drumming replaces nu-metal aggression as defining approach
- White Pony establishes Deftones as critical darlings beyond genre boundaries

## Self-Titled / Saturday Night Wrist Era (2003–2010)

**Albums:** Deftones (2003), Saturday Night Wrist (2006)
**Tours:** Saturday Night Wrist Tour 2006–2007

**Q: What gear did Abe Cunningham use on the self-titled album and Saturday Night Wrist?**
A: Through the self-titled Deftones (2003) and the turbulent Saturday Night Wrist sessions (2006), Cunningham stayed on his Tama Starclassic kit and Pro-Mark sticks while upgrading his cymbal voice to Zildjian K Custom. Chi Cheng's near-fatal car accident (November 2008) cast a shadow over the band heading into what would become Diamond Eyes.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic — maple shells: 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms *(continued — no drum brand change since the 1997 Tama signing)*
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic 14"×6.5" Maple *(continued — warm, full-bodied crack for this era's denser mixes)*
- **Cymbals:** Zildjian K Custom Series — 14" K Custom hi-hats, 16"/18" K Custom crashes, 21" K Custom ride *(upgrade — darker, more refined K Custom palette)*
- **Hardware:** Tama Iron Cobra 900 Rolling Glide *(continued)*
- **Sticks:** Pro-Mark *(continued)*
- **Heads:** Remo Emperor on toms, Powerstroke 3 on kick *(upgrade — Powerstroke 3 for focused kick punch)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$9,000

**Key developments:**
- Deftones (2003) — self-titled album continues the Tama Starclassic setup
- Saturday Night Wrist (2006) — turbulent recording sessions
- Chi Cheng accident (November 2008) — traumatic context heading into Diamond Eyes
- K Custom cymbal upgrade for sophisticated tonal palette

## Diamond Eyes Era (2010–2012)

**Albums:** Diamond Eyes (2010)
**Tours:** Diamond Eyes World Tour 2010–2011

**Q: What gear did Abe Cunningham use on Diamond Eyes?**
A: Diamond Eyes (2010) — written and recorded rapidly in the wake of Chi Cheng's accident — showed Cunningham at his most focused and groove-driven, still on the same Tama Starclassic kit. He moved to a Sabian HHX cymbal setup for the album, a darker, more focused voice he'd carry through the next decade of Deftones records.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic — maple shells: 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms *(continued — no drum brand change accompanied the cymbal switch)*
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic 14"×6.5" Maple *(continued)*
- **Cymbals:** Sabian HHX Series — 14" HHX hi-hats, 16"/18" HHX crashes, 21" HHX ride *(switch from Zildjian K Custom — darker, more focused voice for Diamond Eyes's groove-forward material)*
- **Hardware:** Tama Iron Cobra 900 Rolling Glide *(continued)*
- **Sticks:** Pro-Mark *(continued)*
- **Heads:** Remo Emperor on toms, Powerstroke 3 on kick *(continued)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$10,000

**Key developments:**
- Diamond Eyes (2010) — widely acclaimed; recorded rapidly as a tribute to Chi
- Switched from Zildjian K Custom to Sabian HHX cymbals
- Groove-forward drumming approach reaches full maturity

## Koi No Yokan / Gore / Ohms Era (2012–2022)

**Albums:** Koi No Yokan (2012), Gore (2016), Ohms (2020)
**Tours:** Koi No Yokan Tour 2012–2013, Gore Tour 2016–2017, Ohms Tour 2021–2022

**Q: What gear did Abe Cunningham use across Koi No Yokan, Gore, and Ohms?**
A: Across this span, Cunningham settled into a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit in the Egyptian Night Mist finish and carried his Sabian HHX cymbal setup through all three albums, with his Pro-Mark stick endorsement (since 1997) unchanged. He added a Roland SPD-SX sampling pad to reproduce the layered studio textures of Gore and Ohms in a live context.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga (Egyptian Night Mist) *(switch — broader tonal palette for the Deftones' increasingly cinematic sound)*
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic matching snare *(switch — matches the Bubinga shell; no separate model publicly documented)*
- **Cymbals:** Sabian HHX Series — 14" HHX hi-hats, 18"/20" HHX crashes, 22" HHX ride, 19" HHX China *(continued from the Diamond Eyes era)*
- **Hardware:** Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide Twin Pedal (HP900PTW) + Iron Cobra Lever Glide Hi-Hat (HH905) *(switch — upgraded Iron Cobra platform)*
- **Sticks:** Pro-Mark *(continued — corroborated by DRUM! Magazine, MusicRadar, and Equipboard for this era)*
- **Heads:** Remo Powerstroke P3 / Pinstripe / Ambassador *(upgrade — confirmed by Remo's official artist page)*
- **Electronics:** Roland SPD-SX Sampling Pad *(new — atmospheric triggers for live reproduction of studio layers)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$18,000

**Key developments:**
- Settled into Tama Starclassic Bubinga — Egyptian Night Mist finish
- Koi No Yokan (2012) — most acclaimed Deftones album since White Pony
- Gore (2016) — dark, atmospheric direction; most restrained and sophisticated playing
- Ohms (2020) — pandemic-era cinematic scope; groove sophistication at its peak
- Roland SPD-SX added for live atmospheric textures
- Tama Iron Cobra hardware platform paired with Sabian HHX cymbals

## Zildjian Return Era (2022–Present)

**Albums:** Ohms (2020) touring cycle
**Tours:** Ohms Tour 2021–2022

**Q: What drum kit does Abe Cunningham currently use?**
A: In the years following the Ohms touring cycle, Cunningham returned to a Zildjian setup — pairing brighter A Custom crashes with a darker K Custom ride and hi-hats — after twelve years on Sabian HHX. He remains on his Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit and Tama Iron Cobra hardware, with Pro-Mark sticks and Remo heads unchanged, and supplements the acoustic kit with a Roland SPD-SX sampling pad for live Gore/Ohms textures.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga (Egyptian Night Mist) *(continued — no change from the prior era)*
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic matching snare *(continued)*
- **Cymbals:** Zildjian A Custom / K Custom hybrid — 14" K Custom hi-hats, 18"/20" K Custom crashes, 22" K Custom ride, 19" A Custom China *(switch — returned to a Zildjian hybrid after twelve years on Sabian HHX; debuted a Zildjian Abe Cunningham Artist Series signature stick alongside the cymbal return)*
- **Hardware:** Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide Twin Pedal (HP900PTW) + Iron Cobra Lever Glide Hi-Hat (HH905) *(continued)*
- **Sticks:** Pro-Mark *(continued)*
- **Heads:** Remo Powerstroke P3 / Pinstripe / Ambassador *(continued)*
- **Electronics:** Roland SPD-SX Sampling Pad *(continued)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$18,500

**Key developments:**
- Returned to Zildjian A Custom/K Custom hybrid cymbals after twelve years on Sabian HHX
- Debuted a Zildjian Abe Cunningham Artist Series signature stick (2022)
- Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit and Iron Cobra hardware unchanged

## FAQ

**Q: What drum kit does Abe Cunningham use?**
A: Abe Cunningham plays a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit in the Egyptian Night Mist finish, per Tama's official artist page — the same Tama relationship he's kept since signing in 1997 for Around the Fur, with no drum brand change since. Earlier in his career he used ddrum (through Adrenaline, 1995).

**Q: What cymbals does Abe Cunningham use?**
A: Cunningham currently plays a Zildjian A Custom/K Custom hybrid — 14" K Custom hi-hats, 18"/20" K Custom crashes, a 22" K Custom ride, and a 19" A Custom China — corroborated by DRUM! Magazine, MusicRadar, and Equipboard for the current Deftones era. He built his cymbal voice on Zildjian A through K and K Custom from 1997 through 2010, moved to Sabian HHX for Diamond Eyes (2010) through Ohms (2020), then returned to the Zildjian hybrid in 2022.

**Q: What drums did Abe Cunningham use on White Pony?**
A: On White Pony (2000), Cunningham played the same Tama Starclassic kit he'd used since signing with Tama in 1997 for Around the Fur — no drum brand change accompanied White Pony's more atmospheric sound. He did switch to Zildjian K cymbals during this era, moving on from the brighter A Custom series used on the nu-metal years.

**Q: What gear did Abe Cunningham use on Diamond Eyes?**
A: On Diamond Eyes (2010), Cunningham stayed on his Tama Starclassic kit and moved to a Sabian HHX cymbal setup — a darker, more focused voice he carried through Koi No Yokan, Gore, and Ohms.

**Q: When did Abe Cunningham join the Deftones?**
A: Abe Cunningham is a founding member of the Deftones — he co-founded the band in Sacramento, California in 1988 with Chino Moreno, Stephen Carpenter, and the late Chi Cheng. He was still in high school when the band formed and has appeared on every Deftones studio album from Adrenaline (1995) through Ohms (2020).

**Q: What makes Abe Cunningham's drumming distinctive?**
A: Cunningham is known for his dynamic intelligence and groove sophistication — his ability to serve the song with space, restraint, and precision. Unlike many hard rock drummers, he uses silence and dynamics as compositional tools. His cymbal evolution — from Zildjian A Custom through K Custom, a decade on Sabian HHX, and the 2022 return to a Zildjian hybrid — reflects a consistent pursuit of tones suited to the Deftones' atmospheric direction. See also: [Deftones discography and drumming analysis](/llms/bands.md).

**Q: What snare does Abe Cunningham use?**
A: Cunningham plays a Tama Starclassic matching snare in the modern era, paired with his Starclassic Bubinga kit since 2012. Earlier in his career he used a Tama Starclassic Brass 14"×6.5" snare for White Pony (2000) and a Tama Starclassic maple snare through the Self-Titled, Saturday Night Wrist, and Diamond Eyes years.
