# Blake Richardson Drum Kit Evolution — Complete Timeline

Blake Richardson joined Between the Buried and Me in 2005, replacing Jason Roe, becoming the drummer for one of progressive metal's most critically acclaimed and technically ambitious bands. Formed in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, in 2000, BTBAM has explored the intersection of metal, jazz, hardcore, prog rock, and classical music across an extraordinary catalog. Richardson's drumming is distinguished by its seamless genre vocabulary, dynamic intelligence, and rhythmic invention. This timeline documents his complete gear evolution from the foundational DW/Meinl rig he built for Alaska to the Tama/Sabian configuration he has played since 2018.

## Joining BTBAM / Alaska Era (2005–2006)

**Albums:** Alaska (2005)
**Tours:** Alaska Tour

Blake Richardson joined Between the Buried and Me in 2005, replacing Jason Roe, in time to record Alaska. He built a DW Collector's Series kit paired with Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals — the foundational rig that would anchor BTBAM's sound for over a decade. Richardson's playing on Alaska (2005) began to attract serious critical attention immediately.

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series — all-maple shells: 22"x18" bass drum, 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom (foundational DW kit, built for Alaska and carried through the Colors and Parallax eras)
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple Snare 14"×6.5" (warm, resonant crack for dense arrangements)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" thin crashes, 20" ride, 18" China (dark, hand-hammered Turkish B20 voice established from day one)
- **Hardware:** DW 9002 Chain-Drive Double Bass Pedal (double bass from the earliest recordings)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (settled on in 2006; his consistent stick choice for the rest of his career)
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador clear on toms, coated on snare
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$8,000

**Key developments:**
- Richardson joins BTBAM in 2005, replacing Jason Roe
- Alaska (2005) — first album with Richardson on drums; widely praised for its dynamic range and genre fluency
- Signs with DW, Meinl, and Vic Firth in 2006, establishing the rig used through Colors, The Great Misdirect, the Parallax albums, and Coma Ecliptic

> "We were playing every night. The gear just had to hold together. The music was always more important than what kit I was on." — *Revolver Interview, 2006*

## Colors Era (2007–2009)

**Albums:** Colors (2007)
**Tours:** Colors World Tour 2007–2009

**Q: What drums did Blake Richardson use on Colors?**
A: For Colors (2007), Blake Richardson played the same DW Collector's Series kit and Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals he built for Alaska — no gear change occurred. The warm, all-maple DW tone suited Colors's extraordinarily varied arrangements, which move between extreme metal, jazz, progressive rock, and acoustic passages within a single 65-minute suite.

Colors is widely considered BTBAM's masterpiece and one of progressive metal's greatest albums. Richardson's drumming across the album's single continuous track is extraordinary in its dynamic control and genre vocabulary.

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series (continued) — all-maple shells: 22"x18" bass drum, 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom *(unchanged from Alaska)*
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple Snare 14"×6.5" (continued)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued) — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" thin crashes, 20" ride, 18" China
- **Hardware:** DW 9002 Chain-Drive Double Bass Pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (continued)
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador clear on toms, Remo Powerstroke 3 on kick
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$7,000

**Key developments:**
- No gear change — same DW/Meinl rig from Alaska
- Colors (2007) — career-defining album, widely considered a prog-metal masterpiece
- Colors World Tour builds BTBAM's international fanbase

> "Colors pushed me to think about dynamics in a way no album had before. I had to be a jazz drummer, a death metal drummer, and everything in between — sometimes in the same bar." — *Modern Drummer Interview, 2008*

## The Great Misdirect / Parallax Era (2009–2014)

**Albums:** The Great Misdirect (2009), The Parallax: Hypersleep Dialogues (2011), The Parallax II: Future Sequence (2012)
**Tours:** The Great Misdirect Tour, The Parallax Tour, Progressive Nation at Sea

**Q: What gear did Blake Richardson use from Alaska through The Great Misdirect?**
A: The rig stayed consistent — the DW Collector's Series kit and Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals he built in 2006 for Alaska carried, unchanged, through Colors (2007), The Great Misdirect (2009), and the Parallax albums (2011–2012). A 10" Meinl splash was added during the Parallax era for extra textural range, but no brand transition occurred.

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series (continued) — 22"x18" bass drum, 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple Snare 14"×6.5" (continued — consistent snare across multiple album cycles)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued; 10" splash added for the Parallax albums) — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" thin crashes, 20" ride, 10" splash, 18" China
- **Hardware:** DW 9002 Chain-Drive Double Bass Pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (continued)
- **Heads:** Remo Emperor on toms, Powerstroke 3 on kick *(switch — Powerstroke 3 for more focused kick punch)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$9,000

**Key developments:**
- No brand transition — the DW/Meinl rig from Alaska continues
- Added 10" Meinl splash for expanded textural range during the Parallax era
- The Great Misdirect (2009) — ambitious concept album raises critical profile further
- Parallax II: Future Sequence (2012) — band's most technically demanding release

> "The Byzance cymbals opened up something in my playing. They respond differently — you have to listen more. And that listening made me a better drummer." — *Drum! Magazine, 2012*

## Coma Ecliptic / Automata / Colors II Era (2015–Present)

**Albums:** Coma Ecliptic (2015), Automata I (2018), Automata II (2018), Colors II (2021)
**Tours:** Coma Ecliptic Tour, Automata Tour 2018, Colors II Tour 2021–2022

Coma Ecliptic (2015) was still recorded on the original DW Collector's Series / Meinl Byzance Extra Dry rig. The one real gear transition of Richardson's career came in 2018: he switched to a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit and Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH cymbals for Automata I & II, favoring the Bubinga shells' resonance for BTBAM's Sumerian Records-era production. Colors II (2021) continues on this Tama/Sabian setup.

- **Drums (2015–2017, Coma Ecliptic):** DW Collector's Series (continued, unchanged from Alaska)
- **Drums (2018–present, Automata I & II onward):** Tama Starclassic Bubinga — bubinga shells, custom lacquer finish: double 22"x18" bass drums, 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom *(switch — resonant bubinga tone for Sumerian Records' layered production)*
- **Snare:** Tama STARPHONIC Brass 14"×6.5" *(switch — brass shell for warm, authoritative crack in the Automata era)*
- **Cymbals (2015–2017):** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued)
- **Cymbals (2018–present):** Sabian HHX Evolution / AAX / HH — 14" Dark hi-hats, 17" Extra Dry thin crash, 18" Dark crash, 20" Extra Dry ride, 10" Dark splash, 18" China *(switch from Meinl — full three-series Sabian palette for the Automata era)*
- **Hardware:** Twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals *(switch from the linked DW 9002 — independent per-foot control for Automata's compositional double-bass passages)*
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (unchanged throughout his career)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Coated (snare batter), Evans Hazy 300 (snare resonant) *(switch from Remo on the snare by the Automata era)*
- **Electronics:** Roland SPD-SX Sampling Pad *(new — samples and atmospheric triggers for Colors II's layered studio production in live context)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$8,500

**Key developments:**
- Coma Ecliptic (2015) — still the original DW/Meinl rig; no gear change
- 2018: Switched to Tama Starclassic Bubinga drums and Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH cymbals for Automata I & II
- 2018: Hardware switch from the linked DW 9002 to twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals
- Automata I & II (2018) — science-fiction concept album in two parts
- Colors II (2021) — sequel to the band's masterpiece; continues the Tama/Sabian setup

> "The Starclassic Bubinga is the most musical kit I've played. Everything that comes out of it has this natural warmth. For Colors II, that was exactly what the music needed." — *Tama Drums Artist Profile, 2022*

## FAQ

**Q: What drum kit does Blake Richardson use?**
A: Blake Richardson plays a Tama Starclassic Bubinga — a bubinga shell kit configured with double 22" kick drums and 10", 12", and 13" toms. He played DW (Collector's Series) from 2006 through Coma Ecliptic (2015–2017) before switching to Tama Starclassic Bubinga in 2018 for Automata I & II.

**Q: What cymbals does Blake Richardson use?**
A: Richardson currently plays Sabian cymbals — HHX Evolution hi-hats and crashes, AAX Holy Chinas, and an HH Raw Bell Dry Ride. He switched from Meinl Byzance Extra Dry to Sabian in 2018, around the time of Automata I & II.

**Q: What drums did Blake Richardson use on Colors?**
A: On Colors (2007), Richardson played the same DW Collector's Series kit he built for Alaska (2005) — no gear change occurred for Colors. His cymbal setup was Meinl Byzance Extra Dry during this period; the switch to Sabian came much later, in 2018.

**Q: What gear did Blake Richardson upgrade to after Colors?**
A: After Colors, the DW/Meinl rig remained unchanged through The Great Misdirect (2009–2012) and Coma Ecliptic (2015), aside from a 10" Meinl splash added during the Parallax era. The major gear transition came in 2018, with the move to Tama Starclassic Bubinga drums and Sabian cymbals for Automata I & II.

**Q: What makes Blake Richardson's drumming distinctive in progressive metal?**
A: Richardson is known for exceptional dynamic control and genre fluency — his ability to move between extreme metal, jazz, prog rock, and acoustic textures within a single performance. His cymbal choices (Meinl Byzance Extra Dry, later Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH) reflect a listening-based approach to tone rather than a standard metal palette. See also: [progressive metal drumming techniques](/techniques/progressive-metal).

**Q: Is Blake Richardson a founding member of BTBAM?**
A: No — Between the Buried and Me formed in Winston-Salem, NC in 2000 with founding drummer Will Goodyear (2000-2002). Goodyear was followed by Michael Howard Reig, Mark Castillo, and Jason Roe before Richardson joined in 2005, replacing Roe, and has appeared on every BTBAM studio album from Alaska (2005) through Colors II (2021).

**Q: What double bass pedal does Blake Richardson use?**
A: Richardson used the DW 9002 chain-drive double bass pedal from Alaska (2005) through Coma Ecliptic (2015–2017), then switched to twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals in 2018 for Automata I & II, giving him independent per-foot control for BTBAM's compositional double-bass passages.
