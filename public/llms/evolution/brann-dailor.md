# Brann Dailor Drum Kit Evolution — Complete Timeline

Brann Dailor is one of modern metal's most melodic and technically adventurous drummers, and his gear evolution mirrors Mastodon's rise from Atlanta's underground to progressive metal headliners. He joined the Tama family during the Remission era and has carried a Tama / Meinl / Vater rig, largely unchanged in brand, across two decades with the band. Dailor's jazz-informed approach, constant melodic fills, and compositional drumming require a kit that is as musical as it is powerful.

See also: [Brann Dailor drummer profile](/llms/drummers/brann-dailor.md)

## Remission Era (2000–2003)

**Albums:** Lifesblood EP (2001), Remission (2002)
**Tours:** Early US club touring, Remission US Tour

Mastodon's ferocious debut. Dailor's jazz-influenced, melodic drumming over sludge metal riffs was immediately distinctive, and this era marks his joining the Tama family.

- **Drums:** Tama — 22" kick, 10"/12"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Tama 14"×5" steel — thin hoops
- **Cymbals:** Meinl — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride
- **Hardware:** Tama stands + double pedal (pedal brand not verified for this era)
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B hickory
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear — clear on toms, coated on snare
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$2,800

**Key developments:**
- First Mastodon recording setup
- Jazz-influenced melodic approach applied to sludge metal from day one
- Joined the Tama family during the Remission era

---

## Leviathan Era (2004–2005)

**Albums:** Leviathan (2004)
**Tours:** Leviathan World Tour, Ozzfest 2004

The album that made Mastodon's name worldwide. Dailor signed with Meinl for cymbals during this period, settling into the Mb Series tone that would define his sound for the rest of his career.

- **Drums:** Tama — 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Tama 14"×5.5" steel
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Mb20 & Mb8 Series — 14" hi-hats, 16"/17"/18" crashes, 21" ride, 18" China *(signed with Meinl, 2004)*
- **Hardware:** Tama hardware + double pedal (pedal brand not verified for this era)
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B (continued)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear (continued)
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$5,500

**Key developments:**
- Signed with Meinl for cymbals in 2004
- Ozzfest 2004 brought Mastodon to a mainstream metal audience

---

## Blood Mountain Era (2006–2008)

**Albums:** Blood Mountain (2006)
**Tours:** Blood Mountain World Tour, Sounds of the Underground

Mastodon signs to Reprise/Warner. Dailor continues with the Tama / Meinl / Vater / Evans rig established earlier in his career.

- **Drums:** Tama — 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms (continued)
- **Snare:** Tama 14"×5" steel (continued)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Mb20 & Mb8 Series (continued)
- **Hardware:** Tama hardware + double pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B (continued)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear (continued)
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$9,000

**Key developments:**
- Mastodon signs to Reprise/Warner
- Rig remains consistent with the brands established during the Remission/Leviathan eras

---

## Crack the Skye Era (2009–2010)

**Albums:** Crack the Skye (2009)
**Tours:** Crack the Skye World Tour, Bonnaroo 2009

Widely considered Mastodon's greatest album and Dailor's finest performance. The kit expanded significantly to match the album's progressive ambition.

- **Drums:** Tama — 22" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16"/18" toms *(upgrade — 8" and 18" toms added)*
- **Snare:** Tama 14"×6.5" steel *(upgrade — deeper for a fuller, cinematic sound)*
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Mb20 & Mb8 Series — expanded setup with additional crashes *(upgrade)*
- **Hardware:** Tama hardware + double pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B (continued)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear (continued)
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$14,000

**Key developments:**
- Expanded 6-tom configuration for progressive arrangements
- Deeper snare for fuller, more cinematic tone

---

## The Hunter Era (2011–2013)

**Albums:** The Hunter (2011)
**Tours:** The Hunter World Tour, Rockstar Energy Mayhem Festival 2011

Mastodon's most accessible, rock-oriented album. Dailor streamlined his kit to a tighter 4-tom configuration and chose a shallower snare for a punchier, more direct sound.

- **Drums:** Tama — 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms *(streamlined — 8" and 18" removed)*
- **Snare:** Tama 14"×5" steel *(switch — shallower for sharper, punchier attack)*
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Mb20 & Mb8 Series — streamlined setup for rock context *(switch back)*
- **Hardware:** Tama hardware + double pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B (continued)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear (continued)
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$12,000

**Key developments:**
- Streamlined 4-tom kit for more concise rock arrangements
- Shallower snare returns for punchier, brighter attack
- Mastodon performed at the Grammy Awards 2011 (for "Blood and Thunder")

---

## Once More 'Round the Sun Era (2014–2016)

**Albums:** Once More 'Round the Sun (2014)
**Tours:** Once More 'Round the Sun World Tour, Download Festival 2014

Mastodon returns to heavier and more progressive territory. Dailor's Tama setup moved to the Starclassic Performer line and his hardware relationship with Tama's Speed Cobra pedal line began during this decade.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Performer B/B — 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms *(upgrade)*
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic Performer 14"×6" *(upgrade)*
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Mb20 & Mb8 Series (continued)
- **Hardware:** Tama Speed Cobra double pedal *(signed with Tama hardware, 2010s)*
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B (continued)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear (continued)
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$16,000

**Key developments:**
- Upgrade to Tama Starclassic Performer B/B
- Signed with Tama hardware, using Speed Cobra stands and pedal

## Emperor of Sand Era (2017–2019)

**Albums:** Emperor of Sand (2017)
**Tours:** Emperor of Sand World Tour, Ozzfest Meets Knotfest 2017

Mastodon's most personal album — written while members dealt with family members' cancer battles. The kit expanded again and Emperor of Sand won the Grammy for Best Metal Performance.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Performer B/B — 22" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms *(upgrade — 8" tom re-added)*
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic Performer 14"×6.5" *(upgrade — deeper for fuller body)*
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Mb20 & Mb8 Series — expanded setup (continued)
- **Hardware:** Tama Speed Cobra double pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B (continued)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear (continued)
- **Electronics:** Roland SPD-SX *(new — electronic pad for live samples)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$20,000

**Key developments:**
- Roland SPD-SX added for live electronic integration
- Grammy win for Best Metal Performance

## Hushed and Grim Era (2021–Present)

**Albums:** Hushed and Grim (2021)
**Tours:** Hushed and Grim World Tour 2022, Download Festival 2022

Mastodon's most ambitious work — a double LP tribute to their late manager Nick John. Dailor's most complete and refined setup, with an IEM click track system added to his longstanding Tama / Meinl / Vater / Evans rig.

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Performer B/B (current) — 22" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic Performer 14"×6.5" (current production model)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Mb20 & Mb8 Series (continued)
- **Hardware:** Tama Speed Cobra double pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B (decade-long partnership)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear (continued)
- **Electronics:** Roland SPD-SX + IEM click track system *(upgrade — full IEM integration)*
- **Estimated kit cost:** ~$24,000

**Key developments:**
- Full IEM click track system integrated
- Most refined and complete setup of Dailor's career

## FAQ

**Q: What drum kit does Brann Dailor currently play?**
A: Dailor plays a Tama Starclassic Performer B/B kit — 22" kick drum and 8", 10", 12", 14", and 16" toms. He has played Tama since joining the family during the Remission era (2000).

**Q: What cymbals does Brann Dailor use?**
A: Dailor plays Meinl Mb20 & Mb8 Series cymbals, a partnership that began in 2004.

**Q: When did Brann Dailor join Tama?**
A: Dailor joined the Tama family during the Remission era, around the release of Mastodon's debut album Remission (2002).

**Q: What double pedal does Brann Dailor play?**
A: Dailor plays the Tama Speed Cobra double pedal, part of his Tama hardware endorsement that began in the 2010s. The pedal brand used in his earliest (Remission-era) years is not documented.

**Q: Did Brann Dailor win a Grammy?**
A: Yes — Mastodon won the Grammy Award for Best Metal Performance at the 2018 Grammy Awards for "Sultan's Curse" from Emperor of Sand (2017). Mastodon also performed "Blood and Thunder" at the 2011 Grammy Awards alongside Slash and Esperanza Spalding.
