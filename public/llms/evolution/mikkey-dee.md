# Mikkey Dee Drum Kit Evolution — Complete Timeline

Mikkey Dee holds one of the longest unbroken tenures in metal drumming history: 23 years as Motörhead's drummer, from Bastards in 1993 to Lemmy Kilmister's death in December 2015. His career spans a King Diamond apprenticeship, the full arc of Motörhead's later career as a relentless touring and recording machine, and a post-Motörhead chapter with Scorpions that continues today. His Paiste cymbal endorsement, unbroken since 1987, and his drum progression from Tama through to Sonor SQ2, track nearly four decades of non-stop professional metal drumming. This timeline documents every major equipment era across his career.

## King Diamond Era (1985–1990)

**Albums:** Fatal Portrait (1986), Abigail (1987), Them (1988), Conspiracy (1989), The Eye (1990)
**Tours:** King Diamond world tours 1986–1990, North American and European touring

Mikkey Dee joined King Diamond's band in 1985, recording Fatal Portrait (1986) and Abigail (1987) before leaving in 1988 for a brief stint with Don Dokken's band. He returned to King Diamond as a session player on Conspiracy (1989) and The Eye (1990), before joining Motörhead in 1992. The King Diamond years were his professional apprenticeship: technically demanding melodic metal with theatrical staging requirements, teaching Dee the discipline of consistent performance within complex arrangements. No fixed drum brand endorsement existed yet during this period — his Paiste cymbal endorsement, established in 1987, was the one constant that carried through everything that followed.

- **Drums:** Various kits (no single fixed brand documented) — birch shells: 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms (standard for professional touring drummers of the era; warm, powerful tone suited to King Diamond's melodic metal)
- **Snare:** Stock birch snare 14"×5.5" (sharp, crisp cutting attack across theatrically varied arrangements)
- **Cymbals:** Paiste Signature Series — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride (Dee became a Paiste artist in 1987; bright, cutting, durable — the professional choice for late-1980s hard rock and heavy metal touring)
- **Hardware:** Standard cam-driven double pedal (a step up from basic chain pedals for demanding technical arrangements)
- **Sticks:** Wincent 5B (heavier sticks for the physical demands of consistent touring and recording)
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador (standard single-ply batter heads)
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$5,000

**Key developments:**
- Joined King Diamond's band in 1985
- Paiste Signature cymbal endorsement established (1987)
- Fatal Portrait (1986) and Abigail (1987) — professional debut recordings
- Left for a brief stint with Don Dokken in 1988, returned as a session player on Conspiracy (1989)
- Foundation laid for the non-stop touring career that would define the following three decades

> "King Diamond taught me how to be a professional. The arrangements were demanding, the shows were theatrical — you had to be precise every night. That discipline stuck with me." — *Rhythm Magazine, 2010*

## Early Motörhead Era (1993–2004)

**Albums:** Bastards (1993), Sacrifice (1995), Overnight Sensation (1996), Snake Bite Love (1998), We Are Motörhead (2000)
**Tours:** Motörhead world tours 1993–2004, extensive North American and European touring, Lemmy's 50th birthday tour (1995)

Mikkey Dee joined Motörhead in 1992, replacing Phil Taylor ahead of Bastards (1993, ZYX Music) — the biggest and most demanding role of his career: the sonic avatar of Motörhead's thundering, unrelenting rock 'n' roll. He joined on a Tama kit, and the combination of Tama's punchy character and the band's massive touring schedule drove constant refinement of his live setup. His Paiste cymbals, already in place since 1987, carried over unchanged.

- **Drums:** Tama Artstar II / Swingstar — birch/maple shells: 22"×18" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms *(switch — joined Motörhead on a Tama kit in 1992, first heard on Bastards)*
- **Snare:** Tama steel snare 14"×6.5" *(switch — deeper steel snare for the explosive, walloping crack that became Dee's Motörhead signature)*
- **Cymbals:** Paiste Signature Series — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride (maintained the Paiste relationship established in 1987; bright, cutting character suited Motörhead's raw rock production)
- **Hardware:** DW 5000 Double Pedal — dual-chain drive *(dependable touring hardware that could survive hundreds of shows per year)*
- **Sticks:** Wincent 5B (consistent heavier hickory through the early Motörhead years)
- **Heads:** Remo Emperor / Powerstroke 3 — Emperor on toms, Powerstroke 3 on kick *(upgrade — focused, punchy attack cutting through Motörhead's enormous stage volume)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$7,000

**Key developments:**
- Joined Motörhead — replaced Phil Taylor ahead of Bastards (1993)
- Joined on a Tama Artstar II / Swingstar kit — Paiste cymbals carried over unchanged
- Bastards (1993) debut with band; Overnight Sensation (1996) reaches new commercial peak
- Established one of the most consistent drummer/band combinations in metal history
- Motörhead touring schedule among the most relentless in rock history — up to 200 shows per year

> "With Motörhead, you don't have to think — you just play. Lemmy sets the tempo and the room follows. My job was to be the engine. We played every night like it was the last night." — *Modern Drummer, 2002*

## Late Motörhead / Bad Magic Era (2004–2015)

**Albums:** Kiss of Death (2006), Motörizer (2008), The Wörld Is Yours (2010), Aftershock (2013), Bad Magic (2015)
**Tours:** Motörhead world tours 2004–2015, Download/Wacken/Hellfest headline slots, final Motörhead touring cycle 2015

The final and most refined chapter of Mikkey Dee's Motörhead tenure. Dee's setup reached its most polished form during this period: his Tama kit gave way to a new Sonor SQ2 endorsement in 2012, complete with a Mikkey Dee Signature Snare, a full Evans head partnership, and an expanded Paiste cymbal configuration needed to fill increasingly large arenas and festival stages. Bad Magic won a Grammy for Best Metal Performance ("Thunder & Lightning") — Motörhead's only Grammy, awarded weeks before Lemmy's death on December 28, 2015.

- **Drums:** Tama Artstar II / Swingstar (2004–2012), then Sonor SQ2 — maple/birch hybrid: 22"×18" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms *(switch in 2012 — Sonor SQ2 endorsement signed, fuller, more resonant tone for the arena and festival stages Motörhead now regularly headlined)*
- **Snare:** Sonor SQ2 Mikkey Dee Signature Snare, 14"×7.25" birch *(signature — released alongside the 2012 Sonor SQ2 endorsement; explosive crack remained his defining sound)*
- **Cymbals:** Paiste Signature Series — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" crashes, 21" ride, 19" China *(upgrade — expanded configuration for warmer wash and the biggest production contexts; same Paiste endorsement held since 1987)*
- **Hardware:** DW 5000 Series Double Pedal *(dependable touring hardware for precise, consistent response across hundreds of festival and arena shows per year)*
- **Sticks:** Wincent Mikkey Dee Signature *(signature — custom hickory for the power and projection demands of Motörhead's final era)*
- **Heads:** Evans G2 / EMAD — G2 on toms, EMAD on kick *(switch — warm resonant attack on toms, focused projecting low-end at arena volume)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$18,000

**Key developments:**
- Sonor SQ2 endorsement signed in 2012, replacing Tama as primary kit
- Sonor Mikkey Dee Signature Snare released alongside the 2012 Sonor SQ2 deal
- Evans G2/EMAD heads adopted
- DW 5000 Series Double Pedal continued
- Wincent Mikkey Dee signature sticks launched
- Expanded Paiste cymbal setup for arena and festival stages
- Bad Magic (2015) — Grammy winner ("Thunder & Lightning") — Motörhead's only Grammy
- Lemmy Kilmister dies December 28, 2015 — Motörhead disbands; 23-year tenure ends

> "The last show Motörhead ever played was in Germany. We didn't know it was the last show. That's the way Lemmy would have wanted it — just another night of rock 'n' roll. Everything you had, every night." — *Classic Rock Magazine, 2016*

## Scorpions Era (2016–Present)

**Albums:** Return to Forever (2015, contributed), Rock Believer (2022)
**Tours:** Scorpions world tours 2016–present, farewell tour appearances, stadium and arena headline slots globally

Following Lemmy's death and Motörhead's dissolution, Mikkey Dee joined Scorpions in 2016 — a natural fit given the German band's similar commitment to hard rock fundamentals and relentless touring. His current setup carries over the Sonor SQ2 / Paiste / Evans configuration established in Motörhead's final years, renewed for Scorpions, with a more dynamic snare approach suited to the band's broader dynamic range.

- **Drums:** Sonor SQ2 (continued) — maple/birch hybrid: 22"×18" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms (Sonor SQ2 partnership renewed joining Scorpions in 2016, now optimised for a wider dynamic range than Motörhead's all-out attack demanded)
- **Snare:** Sonor SQ2 Mikkey Dee Signature Snare, 14"×7.25" birch *(continued signature snare, alternated with other Sonor SQ2 configurations depending on setlist demands — a more nuanced approach than the Motörhead years)*
- **Cymbals:** Paiste Signature Series — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" crashes, 21" ride, 19" China (continued Paiste relationship, held since 1987; suited to Scorpions' broader dynamic range from power ballads to hard rock anthems)
- **Hardware:** DW 5000 Series Double Pedal (continued DW partnership; no reason to change a reliable platform)
- **Sticks:** Wincent Mikkey Dee Signature (refined signature model serving both live performance and educational content)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 / EMAD2 — G2 on toms, EMAD2 on kick *(upgrade — tighter, more refined kick tone suited to Scorpions' polished arena production)*
- **Estimated kit cost (original):** ~$22,000

**Key developments:**
- Joined Scorpions (2016) — first major band post-Motörhead
- Rock Believer (2022) — first full Scorpions studio album recorded with Dee from the start
- Sonor SQ2 and Paiste Signature endorsements renewed joining Scorpions
- Evans EMAD2 replaces EMAD for refined low-end
- Continues one of the longest active careers in major metal and hard rock drumming

> "Scorpions is a different world from Motörhead — the dynamics, the ballads, the production values. But the commitment to the show is the same. You give everything every night. That part never changes." — *Rhythm Magazine, 2022*

## FAQ

**Q: What drum kit did Mikkey Dee use in Motörhead?**
A: Mikkey Dee joined Motörhead in 1992 on a Tama Artstar II / Swingstar kit, playing Tama through 2004 and into the start of the late Motörhead / Bad Magic era, before switching to a Sonor SQ2 kit with a Mikkey Dee Signature Snare in 2012 — the setup that carried through the band's final tours and their 2015 Grammy win.

**Q: What drum kit does Mikkey Dee use today?**
A: Mikkey Dee currently plays a Sonor SQ2 kit with Scorpions — a maple/birch hybrid configuration with a 22"×18" kick and 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms, alongside Paiste Signature Series cymbals and the DW 5000 Series Double Pedal.

**Q: What drumsticks does Mikkey Dee use?**
A: Mikkey Dee plays the Wincent Mikkey Dee Signature model to reflect his status as one of the most recognised drummers in hard rock and metal. Before his signature model, he played standard Wincent 5B sticks.

**Q: Why did Mikkey Dee join Scorpions?**
A: Mikkey Dee joined Scorpions in 2016 following Lemmy Kilmister's death in December 2015 and Motörhead's subsequent dissolution. Scorpions' commitment to hard rock fundamentals and relentless touring made it a natural fit for Dee's playing style.

**Q: Did Mikkey Dee win a Grammy with Motörhead?**
A: Yes — Bad Magic (2015) won the Grammy for Best Metal Performance ("Thunder & Lightning"), Motörhead's only Grammy award, given just weeks before Lemmy Kilmister's death on December 28, 2015, which ended the band's run.

**Q: How long was Mikkey Dee Motörhead's drummer?**
A: Mikkey Dee was Motörhead's drummer for 23 years, from Bastards (1993) — his recording debut with the band, after joining in 1992 to replace Phil Taylor — until the band's dissolution following Lemmy Kilmister's death in December 2015.
