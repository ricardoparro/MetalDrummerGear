## Gear Timeline

### Self-Titled / Silent Circus / Alaska Era (1999–2006)

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series — all-maple shells, 22"x18" bass drum, 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple Snare 14"x6.5"
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" thin crashes, 20" ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** DW 9002 Chain-Drive Double Bass Pedal
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A
- **Estimated cost (1999–2006):** ~$8,000
- **Notable:** Richardson built this DW Collector's Series kit with Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals for Alaska (2005), the album that announced BTBAM's progressive metal sound. This DW/Meinl foundation carried through the Colors, The Great Misdirect, and Parallax eras.

### Colors Era (2007–2009)

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series (continued) — all-maple shells, 22"x18" bass drum, 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple Snare 14"x6.5" (continued)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued) — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" thin crashes, 20" ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** DW 9002 Chain-Drive Double Bass Pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (continued)
- **Original setup cost (2007):** ~$7,000
- **Inflation-adjusted to 2026:** ~$10,500
- **Notable:** Colors (2007) is widely considered BTBAM's masterpiece — a single 65-minute continuous track spanning extreme metal, jazz, progressive rock, and acoustic passages. Richardson's DW Collector's Series kit and Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals, unchanged from Alaska, gave the album the warm, dynamic-range-friendly tone it required.

### The Great Misdirect / Parallax Era (2009–2014)

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series (continued) — 22"x18" bass drum, 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple Snare 14"x6.5" (continued)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued, 10" splash added for the Parallax albums) — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" thin crashes, 20" ride, 10" splash, 18" China
- **Pedal:** DW 9002 Chain-Drive Double Bass Pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (continued)
- **Estimated cost (2009):** ~$9,000
- **Notable:** No brand transition happened here — the DW/Meinl rig that debuted on Alaska remained Richardson's setup through The Great Misdirect (2009) and the increasingly technical Parallax albums (2011–2012), with a 10" Meinl splash added for added textural range.

### Coma Ecliptic / Automata / Colors II Era (2015–present)

- **Drums (2015–2017, Coma Ecliptic):** DW Collector's Series (continued, unchanged from the Alaska era)
- **Drums (2018–present, Automata I & II onward):** Tama Starclassic Bubinga — bubinga shells, custom lacquer finish, 22"x18" bass drums (x2), 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom
- **Snare:** Tama STARPHONIC Brass 14"x6.5"
- **Cymbals (2015–2017):** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued)
- **Cymbals (2018–present):** Sabian HHX Evolution / AAX / HH — 14" Dark hi-hats, 17" Extra Dry thin crash, 18" Dark crash, 20" Extra Dry ride, 10" Dark splash, 18" China
- **Pedal:** Twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals (switched from the linked DW 9002 in 2018)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (unchanged throughout his career)
- **Electronics:** Roland SPD-SX Sampling Pad
- **Estimated cost (current):** ~$8,500
- **Notable:** Richardson's most mature era spans one real gear switch, not a Tama-only lineage: Coma Ecliptic (2015) was still recorded on the Alaska-era DW/Meinl rig, and the move to Tama Starclassic Bubinga and Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH came in 2018 for Automata I & II, favoring the Bubinga shells' resonance for BTBAM's Sumerian Records-era production. Colors II (2021) continues on this Tama/Sabian setup.

## Key Gear Changes

- **1999:** BTBAM founded in Winston-Salem, NC
- **2006:** Richardson signs with DW (Collector's Series), Meinl (Byzance Extra Dry), and Vic Firth, building the kit used to record Alaska (2005)
- **2007:** Colors recorded on the same DW/Meinl rig — no gear change
- **2009:** The Great Misdirect / Parallax era — same DW/Meinl rig continues; 10" Meinl splash added
- **2015:** Coma Ecliptic recorded on the same DW/Meinl rig — no gear change
- **2018:** Switch to Tama Starclassic Bubinga drums and Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH cymbals for Automata I & II; pedal switches from the linked DW 9002 to twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals

## FAQ

**Q: How much does Blake Richardson's drum kit cost?**
A: Blake Richardson's 2007 Colors-era DW Collector's Series setup cost approximately $7,000, equivalent to roughly $10,500 today after inflation. His current Tama Starclassic Bubinga setup with Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH cymbals and electronics is valued at approximately $8,500 at current retail.

**Q: What gear does Blake Richardson use?**
A: Blake Richardson plays a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit — a bubinga shell configuration with double 22" kick drums and 10"/12"/13" toms — paired with Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH cymbals, twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals, and Vic Firth American Classic 3A sticks. This has been his setup since 2018, for Automata I & II onward.

**Q: What drums did Blake Richardson use on Colors?**
A: On Colors (2007), Richardson played the same DW Collector's Series kit with Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals he built for Alaska (2005) — no gear change occurred for Colors.

**Q: What was Blake Richardson's first drum kit?**
A: Richardson's foundational setup was a DW Collector's Series kit with Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals, built in 2006 for Alaska (2005) and carried through Colors, The Great Misdirect, the Parallax albums, and Coma Ecliptic, before he switched to Tama Starclassic Bubinga and Sabian in 2018.
