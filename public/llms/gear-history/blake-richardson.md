## Gear Timeline

### Alaska Era (2005–2006)

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series — all-maple shells, 22"x18" kick, 10"/12"/13" rack toms, 16" floor tom
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple 14"x6.5"
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series — 14" hi-hats, 17"/18" crashes, 20" ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** DW 9002 chain-drive double bass pedal
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A
- **Estimated cost (2005–2006):** ~$8,000
- **Notable:** Richardson built this DW/Meinl foundation for Alaska (2005), the album that announced BTBAM's progressive metal sound. The Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals established the dark, hand-hammered tonal character that anchored his setup for over a decade.

### Colors Era (2007–2009)

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series (continued) — same all-maple configuration, refined through two years of touring
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple 14"x6.5" (continued)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued)
- **Pedal:** DW 9002 chain-drive double bass pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (continued)
- **Estimated cost (2007):** ~$8,000 — no change, same kit
- **Notable:** Colors (2007) is widely considered BTBAM's masterpiece — a single 65-minute continuous track spanning extreme metal, jazz, progressive rock, and acoustic passages. Richardson's DW/Meinl setup from Alaska carried unchanged into this era.

### The Great Misdirect / Parallax Era (2009–2014)

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series (continued) — 22"x18" kick, 10"/12"/13" toms, 16" floor tom
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple 14"x6.5" (continued)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued)
- **Pedal:** DW 9002 chain-drive double bass pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (continued)
- **Estimated cost (2009):** ~$8,000 — no change, same kit
- **Notable:** No brand switch occurred in this era. Richardson stayed on the same DW Collector's Series kit and Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals through The Great Misdirect (2009) and the increasingly technical Parallax albums (2011–2012).

### Coma Ecliptic Era (2015–2017)

- **Drums:** DW Collector's Series (continued) — same configuration since Alaska
- **Snare:** DW Collector's Series Maple 14"x6.5" (continued)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Extra Dry Series (continued)
- **Pedal:** DW 9002 chain-drive double bass pedal (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (continued)
- **Estimated cost (2015):** ~$8,000 — no change, same kit
- **Notable:** Coma Ecliptic (2015) was the last BTBAM studio album on the DW/Meinl foundation Richardson built for Alaska a decade earlier, before his 2018 switch to Tama and Sabian.

### Automata / Colors II Era (2018–present)

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga — custom finish, double 22"x18" kick, 10"/12"/13" toms, 16" floor tom
- **Snare:** Tama STARPHONIC 14"x6" Brass
- **Cymbals:** Sabian HHX Evolution / AAX / HH
- **Pedal:** Twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals (not a linked double)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic 3A (unchanged since 2006)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Coated / Hazy 300
- **Estimated cost (current):** ~$9,000
- **Notable:** Richardson switched to a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit and Sabian cymbals in 2018 for Automata I & II — the only drums/cymbals brand change of his career. The setup carried through Colors II (2021) and remains his current rig.

## Key Gear Changes

- **1999:** BTBAM founded in Winston-Salem, NC
- **2006:** Richardson signs with DW (drums + DW 9002 pedal), Meinl (Byzance Extra Dry cymbals), and Vic Firth (American Classic 3A sticks) for the setup built around Alaska (2005)
- **2018:** Switch from DW to Tama Starclassic Bubinga (drums) and from Meinl to Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH (cymbals) for Automata I & II — the only drums/cymbals brand switch of his career
- **2018:** Switch from the DW 9002 chain-drive double pedal to twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals
- **2018:** Switch from Remo to Evans heads (G2 Coated / Hazy 300)

## FAQ

**Q: How much does Blake Richardson's drum kit cost?**
A: Blake Richardson's DW Collector's Series setup from the Alaska through Coma Ecliptic eras (2005–2017) cost approximately $8,000. His current Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit with Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH cymbals, used since Automata I & II (2018), is valued at approximately $9,000 at current retail.

**Q: What gear does Blake Richardson use?**
A: Blake Richardson plays a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit in a custom finish, paired with a Tama STARPHONIC 14"x6" Brass snare, Sabian HHX Evolution/AAX/HH cymbals, twin Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide single pedals, and Vic Firth American Classic 3A sticks.

**Q: What drums did Blake Richardson use on Colors?**
A: On Colors (2007), Richardson played the same DW Collector's Series kit — all-maple shells — and Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals he had built for Alaska (2005). He didn't switch to Tama and Sabian until Automata I & II (2018).

**Q: What was Blake Richardson's first drum kit?**
A: Richardson's foundational setup was a DW Collector's Series kit with Meinl Byzance Extra Dry cymbals, built in 2006 around Alaska (2005) and carried through Colors, The Great Misdirect, the Parallax albums, and Coma Ecliptic before he switched to Tama and Sabian in 2018.
