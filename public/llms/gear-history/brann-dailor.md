## Gear Timeline

### Remission Era (2000–2003)

- **Drums:** Tama — 22" kick, 10"/12"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Tama 14"x5" steel, thin hoops
- **Cymbals:** Meinl — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride
- **Pedal:** Double pedal (brand not verified for this era)
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B hickory
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador — clear on toms, coated on snare
- **Estimated cost (2000–2003):** ~$2,800
- **Notable:** Dailor co-founded Mastodon in Atlanta in 2000 alongside Bill Kelliher, Brent Hinds, and Troy Sanders. Remission (2002) announced his jazz-influenced, melodic drumming approach applied to sludge metal, and this era marks his joining the Tama family.

### Crack the Skye Era (2009–2010)

- **Drums:** Tama (expanded) — 22" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16"/18" toms
- **Snare:** Tama 14"x6.5", deeper for more body
- **Cymbals:** Meinl — 14" hi-hats, dark crashes, 22" ride, China
- **Pedal:** DW 9000 double pedal
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B hickory (continued — no signature deal)
- **Heads:** Remo Coated Ambassador / Fiberskyn
- **Original setup cost (2009):** ~$14,000
- **Inflation-adjusted to 2026:** ~$20,000
- **Notable:** Crack the Skye is widely regarded as Dailor's career-defining masterwork — the title track, "The Czar," and "Oblivion" feature some of the most melodically sophisticated drumming ever recorded on a metal album. This era brought the widest tom configuration of his career, still on the same Tama/Meinl/Vater rig he settled into during Remission.

### Hushed and Grim / Current Era (2021–present)

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Performer B/B — 22" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Tama S.L.P. G-Maple 14"x6.5" (current production model)
- **Cymbals:** Meinl Byzance Series — 15" Byzance Dark hi-hats, 18"/19" crashes, 21" Ghost Ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** Tama Speed Cobra double pedal
- **Sticks:** Vater 5B (continued — no signature deal)
- **Heads:** Evans G2 Clear
- **Estimated cost (current):** ~$24,000
- **Notable:** Hushed and Grim (2021) is Mastodon's first album following the death of longtime manager Nick John, and it carries Dailor's most refined setup yet — the same Tama/Meinl/Vater core he has spent two decades with, now paired with a Tama Speed Cobra double pedal, alongside his rare dual role as drummer and co-lead vocalist.

## Key Gear Changes

- **2000:** Joined the Tama family during the Remission era — career-long drum brand relationship
- **2004:** Signed with Meinl for cymbals during the Leviathan era, for their dark, complex tones
- **2010s:** Signed with Tama hardware, adding the Speed Cobra double pedal and stands
- **2021:** Upgraded to Meinl Byzance Series cymbals for Hushed and Grim's orchestral depth
- **Career constant:** Vater 5B sticks since day one (no signature deal), jazz-influenced melodic tom language, and simultaneous lead/co-lead vocal duties, unmatched among metal drummers

## FAQ

**Q: How much does Brann Dailor's kit cost?**
A: Brann Dailor's 2009 Crack the Skye-era Tama setup cost approximately $14,000. Adjusted for 2026 inflation, that's equivalent to roughly $20,000. His current Tama Starclassic Performer B/B kit with a full Meinl Byzance Series cymbal setup is valued at approximately $24,000 at current retail.

**Q: What drums does Brann Dailor play?**
A: Brann Dailor has played Tama drums since joining the family during Mastodon's Remission era (2002), progressing to his current Tama Starclassic Performer B/B kit, which he has played since the 2010s.

**Q: What cymbals does Brann Dailor use?**
A: Dailor's cymbal setup has been Meinl since he signed with the brand during the Leviathan era (2004) for their dark, complex tones, now expanded to the full Meinl Byzance Series he uses in his current Hushed and Grim-era setup.

**Q: Why is Brann Dailor's drumming considered unique?**
A: Dailor treats his toms as a melodic instrument, tuning them in musical intervals so his constant, flowing fills function as countermelodies rather than punctuation — a jazz-influenced approach rare in metal. He also sings extensive lead and co-lead vocals while executing these technically demanding parts, a combination few drummer-vocalists in any genre can sustain live.

**Q: Has Brann Dailor always played the same drum kit?**
A: Largely, yes — he joined the Tama family for Mastodon's 2002 debut Remission and has stayed with Tama and Meinl, largely unchanged in brand, across two decades with the band, now pairing his Tama Starclassic Performer B/B kit with a Tama Speed Cobra double pedal in his current setup.
