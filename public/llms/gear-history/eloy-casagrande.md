## Gear Timeline

### Sepultura Debut Era (2011–2016)

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga — 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Tama Bell Brass 14"×5.5" (BB146) — bright, cutting attack
- **Cymbals:** Paiste — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** Tama Iron Cobra Double Pedal (chain-drive)
- **Sticks:** Promark hickory, standard (pre-signature era)
- **Heads:** Evans double-ply on toms
- **Original setup cost (2011):** ~$8,000
- **Inflation-adjusted to 2026:** ~$12,000
- **Notable:** Casagrande joined Sepultura in 2011 at just 16 years old, replacing Jean Dolabella — one of the most remarkable early-career signings in metal history. His Tama endorsement began immediately with the band, giving him the reliable foundation needed for his first years of sustained international touring. He was already a Paiste artist, having signed with the cymbal brand in 2005 at age 17, six years before joining Sepultura. The Mediator Between Head and Hands Must Also Be the Conductor (2013) announced his arrival as a world-class metal drummer.

### Machine Messiah / Quadra Era (2017–2022)

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga — expanded configuration; 22" kick, 8"/10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Tama Bell Brass 14"×5.5" (BB146) — consistent across the entire Sepultura tenure
- **Cymbals:** Paiste — expanded setup; 14" hi-hats, 16"/18"/19" crashes, 21" ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** Tama Iron Cobra Double Pedal (consistent)
- **Sticks:** Promark hickory
- **Heads:** Evans on toms, Evans kick head
- **Electronics:** Roland SPD-SX sampling pad — new for Quadra's layered studio arrangements in the live context
- **Original setup cost (2017):** ~$14,000
- **Inflation-adjusted to 2026:** ~$16,000
- **Notable:** Machine Messiah (2017) is widely considered Casagrande's defining Sepultura album — a dramatically more sophisticated musical palette combining Sepultura's Brazilian rhythmic identity with progressive metal structures. Quadra (2020), a conceptual double album, and the acoustic SepulQuarta (2021) are considered the high-water mark of his Sepultura work, showcasing his dynamic range far beyond typical extreme metal drumming. His expanded Tama Starclassic Bubinga and Paiste setup grew to match these compositional ambitions.

### Slipknot Era (2023–present)

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga — dual bass drum configuration; 22"×16" and 24"×14" bass drums, 10", 12", 13" toms, 16" and 18" floor toms
- **Snare:** Tama Bell Brass 14"×5.5" (BB146) — the snare that has followed Eloy from Sepultura to Slipknot
- **Cymbals:** Paiste — expanded configuration; 15" Masters Dark hi-hats, 20" Masters Dark ride, dual 20" 602 crashes, 10" Rude splash, 20" Masters Dark crash, 20" 2002 Heavy ride, 20" 2002 Novo China, 10" 2002 Mega Bell, Symphonic Gong
- **Pedal:** Tama Iron Cobra Double Pedal (consistent)
- **Sticks:** Promark Eloy Casagrande Signature — custom model launched to coincide with the Slipknot chapter
- **Heads:** Evans on toms, Evans on kick
- **Electronics:** Roland TD module + SPD-SX — full hybrid setup for Slipknot's layered production
- **Original setup cost (2023):** ~$25,000
- **Notable:** Casagrande was announced as Slipknot's new drummer in 2023, replacing Jay Weinberg. Rather than switching brands for the new chair, he maintained his existing Tama, Paiste, and Evans endorsements, now scaled for stadium and arena-level performance — his Tama Starclassic Bubinga expanded to a dual-kick configuration, and his Paiste setup grew to cover the full dynamic range of Slipknot's catalog.

## Key Gear Changes

- **2011:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga — first professional drum endorsement, immediate upon joining Sepultura
- **2005:** Signed as a Paiste artist at age 17, six years before joining Sepultura — the longest-running partnership in his career
- **2017–2022:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga and Paiste cymbal configurations both expanded for Machine Messiah and Quadra's broader dynamic range
- **~2020:** Roland SPD-SX sampling pad added for Quadra-era live electronics
- **2023:** Promark Eloy Casagrande Signature sticks launched, coinciding with the Slipknot chapter — replacing standard Promark hickory
- **2023:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga expanded to a dual bass drum configuration and Paiste cymbal setup expanded again for Slipknot's stadium-scale productions and catalog breadth
- **Consistency:** Tama drums, Tama Bell Brass snare, Tama Iron Cobra pedal, Paiste cymbals, and Evans heads have run unbroken across his entire career — Sepultura and Slipknot alike

## FAQ

**Q: What drum kit does Eloy Casagrande use?**

His Slipknot rig uses a dual bass drum configuration, paired with a Tama Bell Brass snare, Paiste cymbals, and a Tama Iron Cobra double pedal.

**Q: What brand does Eloy Casagrande endorse?**  
A: Eloy Casagrande endorses Tama drums (since 2011), Paiste cymbals (since 2005 — his longest-running partnership), Promark sticks (signature model since 2023), and Evans drumheads. All four endorsements have carried consistently from his Sepultura tenure into his current Slipknot role.

**Q: What is Eloy Casagrande's most expensive piece of gear?**  
A: Eloy Casagrande's most expensive piece of gear is his Tama Starclassic Bubinga dual-kick drum kit for Slipknot, part of a complete touring rig estimated at approximately $25,000 including his expanded Paiste cymbal configuration and Roland electronics.

**Q: Why did Eloy Casagrande keep the same gear brands after joining Slipknot?**  
A: Eloy Casagrande maintained his existing Tama, Paiste, and Evans endorsements after joining Slipknot in 2023 rather than switching brands — a sign of how strong those relationships were after over a decade with Sepultura. His setup was instead scaled up to meet Slipknot's stadium and arena touring demands: a dual bass drum Tama configuration and a significantly expanded Paiste cymbal array to cover the band's catalog.
