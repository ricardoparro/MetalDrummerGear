# Frost Drum Kit Gear History — Satyricon / 1349

## Gear Timeline

### Raw Black Metal / Corpse Paint Era (1993–1999)

- **Drums:** Pearl Export Series — birch/poplar shells, 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Pearl Export 14"x5.5" steel
- **Cymbals:** Paiste 2002 Series — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride
- **Pedal:** Basic single pedal, standard stands
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador
- **Estimated cost (1993–1999):** ~$1,800
- **Notable:** Frost joined Satyricon in 1993, entering Norway's second-wave black metal scene. Nemesis Divina (1996) established the primitive, hyperspeed blast beat foundation on an affordable, entry-level Pearl Export kit — budgets were thin and corpse paint mattered more than gear endorsements.

### Industrial Black Metal / Founding 1349 Era (1999–2006)

- **Drums:** Pearl Export Series (continued) — birch/poplar shells, 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Pearl Export 14"x5.5" steel
- **Cymbals:** Paiste 2002 Series (continued) — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride
- **Pedal:** Double pedal with kick trigger — electronic trigger augmentation added
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Powerstroke 3 (kick), Ambassador (toms)
- **Estimated cost (1999–2006):** ~$4,500
- **Notable:** Rebel Extravaganza (1999) pushed Satyricon into industrial black metal territory. Frost remained on his Pearl Export/Paiste 2002 setup — the brand switch wouldn't come until 2013 — but added electronic kick-trigger augmentation and upgraded to heavier-duty sticks and reinforced heads. He joined 1349 around 2000, becoming a full member by 2003; Liberation (2003) and Hellfire (2005) showcased some of the fastest, most disciplined blast beat drumming the genre had produced.

### Now, Diabolical Era (2006–2013)

- **Drums:** Pearl Export Series (continued) — birch/poplar shells, 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Pearl Export 14"x5.5" steel
- **Cymbals:** Paiste 2002 Series (continued) — 14" hi-hats, 16"/18" crashes, 20" ride
- **Pedal:** Double pedal with kick trigger (continued)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Powerstroke 3 (kick), Ambassador (toms)
- **Original setup cost (2006):** ~$7,500
- **Inflation-adjusted to 2026:** ~$11,500
- **Notable:** Now, Diabolical (2006) brought Satyricon to its commercial peak, with Frost's drumming evolving toward a more song-serving, almost rock-inflected sensibility without sacrificing extremity. He remained on the same Pearl Export/Paiste 2002 setup throughout this era — the switch to Tama and Zildjian wouldn't happen until the 2013 self-titled album.

### Experimental Maturity / Current Setup (2013–present)

- **Drums:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga Series — bubinga shells, 22" kick, 10"/12"/14"/16" toms
- **Snare:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga 14"x6", bubinga shell, dry cutting tone
- **Cymbals:** Zildjian A Series — standard A Series setup
- **Pedal:** Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide Pedal + Iron Cobra Lever Glide Hi-Hat Stand
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Powerstroke 3 (kick), Ambassador (toms)
- **Original setup cost (2013):** ~$8,500
- **Inflation-adjusted to 2026:** ~$9,800
- **Notable:** By the self-titled Satyricon (2013), Frost's playing had become a study in restraint and dynamics, and he switched to a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit with Zildjian A Series cymbals and the Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide pedal + Lever Glide hi-hat stand — the first gear-brand change of his career. This setup reached its fully refined, definitive configuration for Deep calleth upon Deep (2017) and 1349's The Infernal Pathway (2019) — widely cited as one of extreme metal's most disciplined and influential drummers.

## Key Gear Changes

- **1999:** Rebel Extravaganza pushes Satyricon into industrial territory; electronic kick-trigger augmentation added to the live rig, Pearl/Paiste setup unchanged
- **2001:** Joins 1349 as drummer, appearing on the band's debut EP — becomes a full member by 2003
- **2006:** Now, Diabolical becomes Satyricon's commercial breakthrough while Frost remains on his Pearl Export/Paiste 2002 setup
- **2013:** Switches from Pearl Export/Paiste 2002 to Tama Starclassic Bubinga / Zildjian A Series, and upgrades to the Tama Iron Cobra Power Glide pedal + Lever Glide hi-hat stand — the first gear-brand change of his career
- **2017:** Tama Starclassic Bubinga / Zildjian A Series configuration reaches its fully refined, career-defining form
- **Career constant:** Vic Firth stick brand (5B, then American Classic Extreme 5B) and Remo head setup, prioritizing reliability at extreme blast-beat tempos across three decades

## FAQ

**Q: How much does Frost's drum kit cost?**
A: Frost's 2006 Now, Diabolical-era Pearl Export/Paiste 2002 setup cost approximately $7,500. Adjusted for 2026 inflation, that's equivalent to roughly $11,500. His current Tama Starclassic Bubinga / Zildjian A Series setup, adopted in 2013, is valued at approximately $9,800 at current retail.

**Q: What drums does Frost play?**
A: Frost has played a Tama Starclassic Bubinga Series kit since 2013 — his current setup for Satyricon and 1349. Before that he played a Pearl Export kit from 1993 to 2013, carried through the Nemesis Divina, Rebel Extravaganza, and Now, Diabolical eras, before switching to Tama alongside Satyricon's 2013 self-titled album.

**Q: What cymbals does Frost use?**
A: Frost's cymbal setup was Paiste 2002 from 1993 to 2013, then switched to Zildjian A Series alongside Satyricon's 2013 self-titled album — a standard A Series setup he has maintained since.

**Q: Why is Frost's drumming considered so influential in black metal?**
A: Frost treats blast beats as architectural events rather than pure texture — shaping dynamic swells, deliberate cymbal voicing, and micro-variations in foot pattern that make even the densest material feel kinetic. He has balanced two demanding bands, Satyricon and 1349, across three decades while setting the technical benchmark for Norwegian black metal drumming.

**Q: Has Frost always played the same drum kit?**
A: No — he started on a budget Pearl Export kit for Satyricon's early years and remained on that same Pearl Export/Paiste 2002 setup through Rebel Extravaganza, the founding of 1349, and Now, Diabolical (2006), before switching to a Tama Starclassic Bubinga kit with Zildjian A Series cymbals alongside Satyricon's self-titled album in 2013 — the only gear-brand change of his career.
