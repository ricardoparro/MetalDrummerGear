# Inferno Drum Kit Gear History — Behemoth

## Gear Timeline

### Early Career / Black Metal Era (1997–2000)

- **Drums:** Developing Pearl kits — standard production shells, double bass configuration, no flagship endorsement yet
- **Snare:** Pearl steel/maple snare 14"
- **Cymbals:** Developing cymbal setup — mixed brands before establishing the Paiste RUDE endorsement
- **Pedal:** Standard double pedal (entry-level hardware)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador
- **Notable:** Inferno joined Behemoth in 1997, replacing Baal Ravenlock, as the band transitioned from raw black metal toward a more technical death metal approach. His early setups were practical working drummer rigs — developing Pearl shells but not prestige endorsement gear. The focus was on endurance and reliability across Eastern European touring circuits.

### Satanica / Thelema.6 Era (1999–2000)

- **Drums:** Developing Pearl kits — same entry-level configuration, no fixed endorsement yet
- **Snare:** Pearl steel/maple snare 14"
- **Cymbals:** Developing cymbal setup — mixed brands before establishing the Paiste RUDE endorsement
- **Pedal:** Standard double pedal (entry-level hardware)
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Ambassador (toms), Remo Powerstroke P3 (kick)
- **Notable:** Satanica (1999) marked a turning point — Behemoth's sound became denser and more technically demanding, and Inferno's equipment preferences continued developing ahead of the flagship Pearl Reference Series / Paiste RUDE endorsement that would arrive with "Demigod."

### Zos Kia Cultus / Demigod Era (2002–2004)

- **Drums:** Pearl Reference Series — 6-piece; 22"x18" kick, 10"x8" rack, 12"x9" rack, 14"x14" floor, 16"x16" floor
- **Snare:** Pearl Reference 14"x5.5"
- **Cymbals:** Paiste RUDE Series — 14" Hi-Hats, 14" Blast China, 18" & 19" Crashes, 24" Mega Power Ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** Czarcie Kopyto (Devil's Hoof) double pedal
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Powerstroke P3 / Emperor
- **Original setup cost (2004):** ~$4,800
- **Inflation-adjusted to 2026:** ~$8,329
- **Notable:** Demigod (2004) is considered Behemoth's technical peak and the album that brought Inferno international recognition. The Pearl Reference Series shell construction gave maximum projection for extreme tempos. The Paiste RUDE cymbal upgrade transformed his sound — the aggressive, cutting tonal character pierced through Behemoth's heavily distorted guitar wall at blast-beat speeds.

### Apostasy / Evangelion Era (2007–2009)

- **Drums:** Pearl Reference Series (continued) — same shell configuration
- **Snare:** Pearl Reference 14"x5.5"
- **Cymbals:** Paiste RUDE Series (full set) — 14" Hi-Hats, 14" Blast China, 18" & 19" Crashes, 24" Mega Power Ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** Czarcie Kopyto (Devil's Hoof) double pedal
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Powerstroke P3 / Emperor (continued)
- **Notable:** Apostasy (2007) and Evangelion (2009) saw Inferno refine rather than replace his setup. The Paiste RUDE series became his defining cymbal identity — aggressive and cutting, capable of maintaining definition at extreme playing speeds where darker cymbals would blur into noise.

### The Satanist Era (2014)

- **Drums:** Pearl Reference Series — Pearl's flagship line, double bass drums triggered for consistency
- **Snare:** Pearl Reference 14"x5.5" Brass/Steel
- **Cymbals:** Paiste RUDE Series (same configuration)
- **Pedal:** Czarcie Kopyto (Devil's Hoof) double pedal
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo Powerstroke P3 Clear (toms), Remo Emperor X (snare)
- **Notable:** The Satanist (2014) is Behemoth's most commercially successful album and features some of Inferno's most musically nuanced drumming. His Pearl Reference Series kit delivered the enhanced sustain and tonal depth critical for the album's wider dynamic range, which moves from brutal blast beats to slower, atmospheric passages.

### Current Setup (2015–present)

- **Drums:** Pearl Reference Series — same flagship line, ongoing endorsement
- **Snare:** Pearl Reference 14"x5" Steel
- **Cymbals:** Paiste RUDE Series — 14" Hi-Hats, 14" Blast China, 18" & 19" Crashes, 24" Mega Power Ride, 18" China
- **Pedal:** Czarcie Kopyto (Devil's Hoof) double pedal
- **Sticks:** Vic Firth American Classic Extreme 5B
- **Heads:** Remo (ongoing)
- **Notable:** Inferno's current setup reflects over two decades of Pearl and Paiste endorsement stability. The Paiste RUDE cymbals — engineered for durability and cut under extreme playing — remain the defining element of his ongoing quest for precision at extreme tempos.

## Key Gear Changes

- **1997–2003:** Developing Pearl kits — working drummer's practical choice before a fixed endorsement
- **2004:** Pearl Reference Series + Paiste RUDE Series established — the endorsement combination that has defined his sound for two decades
- **2004:** Czarcie Kopyto (Devil's Hoof) double pedal adopted for direct-drive speed and response
- **2014–present:** Pearl Reference Series / Paiste RUDE Series rig confirmed as his settled, long-term platform

## FAQ

**Q: What drum kit does Inferno use?**  
A: Inferno currently plays a Pearl Reference Series, Pearl's flagship kit line, anchored by a Pearl Reference 14"x5" Steel snare. He pairs it with Paiste RUDE cymbals, a Czarcie Kopyto (Devil's Hoof) double pedal, and Remo drumheads. He has been a Pearl endorser since the "Demigod" era (2004).

**Q: What drums did Inferno use on Demigod?**  
A: On Demigod (2004), Inferno used a Pearl Reference Series shell pack with Paiste RUDE Series cymbals and a Czarcie Kopyto (Devil's Hoof) double pedal. The complete setup cost approximately $4,800, equivalent to about $8,329 adjusted for 2026 inflation.

**Q: What cymbals does Inferno (Behemoth) use?**  
A: Inferno has been a Paiste endorser since the "Demigod" era, and specifically plays the RUDE Series: 14" Hi-Hats, a 14" Blast China, 18" and 19" Crashes, a 24" Mega Power Ride, and an 18" China. Their aggressive, cutting tone pierces through Behemoth's heavily distorted guitar wall rather than getting buried beneath it.

**Q: What double bass pedals does Inferno use?**  
A: Inferno has used a Czarcie Kopyto (Devil's Hoof) double pedal — a Polish-engineered direct-drive mechanism — since the "Demigod" era, seated on a Pearl D-2000 throne. The direct-drive action gives him the precise, high-speed double-kick response central to Behemoth's extreme metal style.

**Q: When did Inferno join Behemoth?**  
A: Inferno joined Behemoth in 1997, replacing Baal Ravenlock, as the band transitioned from raw black metal toward technical death metal. He has been Behemoth's drummer for the entirety of their commercial period, appearing on all studio albums from Satanica (1999) onward.
