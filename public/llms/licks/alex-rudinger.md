## Autotheist Movement III: Deconsecrate — Technical Fill Vocabulary

**Song:** Autotheist Movement III: Deconsecrate | **Album:** Autotheism (2012) | **BPM:** ~190 BPM | **Technique:** fill techniques | **Difficulty:** expert

"Autotheist Movement III: Deconsecrate" is the closing movement of The Faceless's Autotheism (2012), an album that pushed the band's technical death metal sound into more progressive and experimental territory, blending extreme metal with clean vocals, saxophone, and carnival-esque tonal shifts. Alex Rüdinger played drums on this era of the band during his 2013-2014 tenure, bringing the high-precision, rhythmically complex vocabulary that has defined his career across The Faceless, Conquering Dystopia, and later Trivium. "Deconsecrate" is a demanding showcase for that vocabulary: the track shifts constantly between blast-beat intensity, technical double-bass patterns, and abrupt tempo and meter changes that require a drummer to track the song's structure as closely as its individual hits. Rüdinger's approach here reflects the discipline he built starting his professional career at 18, filling stints in bands like Threat Signal before landing in the technical death metal world — the ability to absorb a highly demanding part quickly and execute it with control rather than just raw speed. The song's sudden shifts from full-band chaos into sparse, exposed drum fills demand precise hand-foot coordination and total command of dynamics, since a fill that is too loose or a tempo change that isn't locked in breaks the arrangement's cohesion. For drummers, "Deconsecrate" is an excellent study in technical death metal's fill vocabulary specifically because it isn't just fast — it's structurally unpredictable, forcing the player to internalize the song's form rather than relying on muscle memory alone. Learning it develops blast-beat-to-fill transitions under tempo changes, the coordination needed for rapid double-bass runs integrated with off-beat snare and tom patterns, and the mental mapping required to navigate a song that shifts direction as often as this one does. It is a fitting introduction to the technical rigor that has made Rüdinger a first-call session and touring drummer across progressive and technical metal, culminating in his 2025 role as Trivium's touring and studio drummer.
