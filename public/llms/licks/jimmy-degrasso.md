## 1000 Times Goodbye — Driving Verse & Chorus Groove

**Song:** 1000 Times Goodbye | **Album:** The World Needs a Hero (2001) | **BPM:** ~157 BPM | **Technique:** main groove | **Difficulty:** intermediate

"1000 Times Goodbye" from Megadeth's The World Needs a Hero (2001) is one of the clearest showcases of what Jimmy DeGrasso brought to the band during his 1998-2002 tenure: a hard-hitting, arena-tested pocket sense built across years in Y&T, Suicidal Tendencies, and Alice Cooper before he ever sat behind Megadeth's kit. The World Needs a Hero was a deliberate return to a heavier sound after the more experimental Risk (1999), and DeGrasso's drumming across the album reflects that shift — direct, powerful, and built to serve Dave Mustaine's riffs rather than draw attention away from them. On "1000 Times Goodbye", running at roughly 157 BPM, DeGrasso locks a driving backbeat to the song's alternating heavy and melodic sections, keeping the energy consistent through the verse-chorus structure while adjusting his kick patterns to match each part's intensity. What stands out is the reliability and weight of his playing: DeGrasso built his career as a first-call drummer precisely because he could walk into a new band's back catalogue on short notice — as he famously did joining Megadeth in 1998 — and deliver a powerful, song-serving performance without needing to reinvent the part. His backbeat here is consistently hard-hitting, his double-bass work is clean and controlled rather than flashy, and his cymbal choices reinforce the song's momentum without cluttering the mix. For drummers, this track is a strong study in adapting a pocket-first, hard rock foundation to thrash metal's demands: it rewards a player who can hit hard and stay locked to the riff rather than one chasing technical display. Learning the groove develops backbeat consistency at a driving tempo, the ability to shift dynamic intensity between song sections without losing time, and the practical, professional mindset that let DeGrasso move seamlessly between Y&T's hard rock, Suicidal Tendencies' crossover thrash, and Megadeth's precision-driven Big Four sound. "1000 Times Goodbye" remains a good entry point into DeGrasso's studio work with Megadeth and a useful lesson in playing with authority and consistency rather than complexity.
