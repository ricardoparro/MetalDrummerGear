## Playing Style

Paul Mazurkiewicz co-founded Cannibal Corpse in Buffalo, New York in 1988 and has anchored every studio album the band has released since their 1990 debut "Eaten Back to Life" — a 35-plus-year tenure that makes him one of the most consistent drummers in death metal, driving landmark records like "Tomb of the Mutilated" (1992) and "The Bleeding" (1994) with thunderous, song-serving brutality. Pete Sandoval joined Morbid Angel in 1988, recording the genre-defining "Altars of Madness" (1989) before also co-founding grindcore pioneers Terrorizer, where his blast beat work on "World Downfall" (1989) helped define extreme metal drumming across two bands simultaneously.

## Technique

Mazurkiewicz uses a traditional alternating-leg double kick approach, prioritizing consistency, groove, and relentless drive over technical flash — his parts serve Cannibal Corpse's riffs rather than showcasing individual virtuosity, keeping tempos punishing across marathon brutal death compositions. Sandoval pioneered the gravity blast, a technique that harnesses gravity and stick rebound rather than muscle to generate single-footed blast beats at speeds once considered physically impossible, a discovery AllMusic's Jason Birchmeier said "challenges one's perception of how fast a drummer can possibly drum."
